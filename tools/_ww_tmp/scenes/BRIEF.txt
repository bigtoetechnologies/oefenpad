WERKWOORDEN — SCENE BRIEF FOR ONE PAGE
=====================================

Each card in Docs/werkwoorden/ stands for one Dutch verb in its base form. The
card is a picture brief: one drawing, in the same cobalt-on-white style as
Docs/voorzetselpaden/, that makes the verb's meaning obvious. There are no
example sentences and no conjugation material on these cards. The drawing has
to carry the verb on its own.

Write one row per verb, in the exact order of your page TSV.


THE FIELDS
----------
raw values, tab-separated, five columns, header row first:

  verb<TAB>scene<TAB>must<TAB>avoid<TAB>alt

verb   the infinitive, copied exactly from your page TSV.
scene  English. What is drawn, as a brief for the illustrator. 35–55 words.
       Say who, what they do, and where. Name every object that must be
       visible. Always a person ("one adult", "two adults a metre apart",
       "a child"), never "someone".
must   English. The single thing that must be readable at thumbnail size, in
       one short clause ending in a full stop. One mark, not a list.
avoid  English. Three to five near-miss actions, separated by semicolons, each
       with the competing verb in brackets. This is what stops the drawing
       turning into a neighbour. Example:
         both feet off the ground and the body leaning forward (rennen);
         a raised knee and a straight swinging arm (marcheren);
         a walking stick; speed lines or dust puffs.
alt    Dutch. One sentence for a screen reader, at most 20 words. Describe the
       picture; never name the verb, and never use a form of it. If the
       picture is of two people passing a box, write "Twee mensen staan
       tegenover elkaar met een doos tussen hun handen." — not "geven".

No tab characters and no line breaks inside a field. No double quotes. Plain
hyphens only.


WHAT MAKES A GOOD SCENE
-----------------------
- One action, caught at its clearest moment. A verb is a moment in time, not a
  pose: the box is halfway between two pairs of hands, the foot is lifting off.
- Every object named must be drawable as one bold shape at 170 px wide. A
  bicycle, a kettle, a key, a spade, a letter: fine. A crowd, a city, weather:
  no.
- People look at the thing they are acting on. Two people who interact face
  each other.
- Say what is NOT in the picture when the frame would otherwise be busy: "no
  second person, no furniture, nothing else in the picture."
- The scene has to be telling apart from its neighbours. Read the avoid lists
  on your whole page before you write: no two verbs may end up with the same
  action.
- Keep the register of the level. A0 verbs are body, food, home, moving about.
  A2 and B1 verbs may need an office, a shop, a classroom, a hospital, a
  building site.


ABSTRACT VERBS — A CLOSED LIST OF CONVENTIONS
---------------------------------------------
A drawing cannot show "to be able to". For the core A0 and A1 verbs only, a
conventional pictogram is allowed, because it is the one a learner already
knows from signage and cartoons:

  thought bubble with a question mark   thinking, wondering
  thought bubble with a bright lightbulb  knowing, having an idea
  two pans on a balance                 weighing, judging
  a single bold question mark over a head  not knowing
  a raised arm with a flexed fist       being able to
  a heavy weight pressing down on a back   having to
  both hands reaching for an object behind glass  wanting
  an arrow pointing forward past a doorway  the future, will

Rules for these:
- Only for A0 and A1. Say the words "This is the conventional metaphor for ..."
  in the scene so a reviewer can see the card is not a literal picture.
- One symbol only, drawn bold, and nothing else symbolic in the frame.
- Never write letters, numbers or words in the drawing.
- The symbol must differ from the symbol used for the neighbouring abstract
  verb on the same page. Denken gets the bubble with a question mark, weten
  gets the bubble with a lightbulb; they may not both use a bubble.

Beyond A0 and A1, an abstract or figurative verb is NOT drawn. Write:

  verb<TAB>SKIP: <one sentence why><TAB>-<TAB>-<TAB>-

Use SKIP for metaphors that only work in Dutch idiom (ontbreken, blijken,
gelden, heten, schijnen, lijken, behoren) rather than inventing a symbol, and
for any verb whose only picture would be the picture of another verb on the
same page.


EXAMPLES
--------
These eight are already written and are the standard to match. Four are
literal, two use an allowed convention, one is a two-person action, one is a
SKIP.

lopen   A0 irregular, rank 292
scene  one adult walks from left to right over empty ground, caught
       mid-stride: the front foot flat on the ground, the back foot just
       lifted on its toes, the arms swinging in opposition, back straight,
       eyes ahead. Nothing else in the picture.
must   an ordinary unhurried walk, one foot down and one just leaving the
       ground.
avoid  both feet off the ground and the body leaning forward (rennen); a
       raised knee and a straight swinging arm (marcheren); a person standing
       still beside a bicycle (fietsen); a walking stick; speed lines or dust
       puffs.
alt    Een volwassene is midden in een stap: de voorste voet staat plat op de
       grond, de achterste voet is net van de vloer.

geven   A0 irregular, rank 112
scene  two adults stand facing each other an arm's length apart. The person
       on the left holds out a closed cardboard box with both hands; the
       person on the right has both hands already closed around the far side
       of the box, so only hands and box touch. Both look at the box.
must   a box passing from hand to hand between two people who face each other.
avoid  one person alone pressing a box to the chest (hebben); an open empty
       palm held out (vragen); coins and notes on a counter (betalen); a box
       with a ribbon and bow (cadeau); one hand lifted in a wave (zwaaien).
alt    Twee mensen staan tegenover elkaar met een doos tussen hun handen.

denken   A0 irregular, rank 194 — allowed convention
scene  one adult sits on a plain chair, chin on one fist, that elbow on the
       knee, eyes looking up and away. Above and beside the head floats a
       conventional thought bubble: three small circles rising to one large
       cloud outline, and inside the cloud a single bold question mark. This
       is the conventional metaphor for thinking; draw it plainly and add no
       other symbol.
must   a seated person with a thought bubble and a question mark above the
       head.
avoid  a light bulb inside the bubble (weten); a book and a pen (studeren);
       two people with speech bubbles (praten); a bare question mark floating
       with no head beneath it; a hand pressed to the mouth (twijfelen).
alt    Iemand zit op een stoel met de kin op de vuist; boven het hoofd hangt
       een gedachtewolk met een vraagteken.

zijn   A0 irregular, rank 1 — allowed convention
scene  one adult stands facing the viewer, feet apart and planted, and lays
       an open palm flat on their own chest; the other arm hangs relaxed at
       the side. The figure stands alone in the middle of the frame. This is
       the conventional "this is me" gesture for being; draw it plainly and
       put nothing else in the picture.
must   a standing person with an open palm on their own chest.
avoid  a hand pointing away at someone else (wijzen); a hand pressed to the
       stomach (buikpijn); arms folded across the chest (boos); a name tag,
       badge or any lettering; both hands raised.
alt    Iemand staat recht voor de kijker met een open handpalm op de eigen
       borst.

maken   A0 regular, rank 52
scene  one adult sits at a plain table, holding a small wooden box together
       with both hands; a hammer lies beside three loose planks and a few
       nails, and one nail stands half driven into the box. The box is clearly
       being assembled out of the planks.
must   a new box being made from loose planks and nails.
avoid  a screwdriver and a broken chair leg (repareren); a pot on a stove and
       a spoon (koken); a brush on paper (tekenen); the finished box held up
       to show it.
alt    Iemand zit aan een tafel en zet met een hamer een kistje in elkaar uit
       losse planken.

werken   A0 regular, rank 152
scene  one adult sits at a plain desk seen from the side, both hands on the
       keyboard of an open laptop, eyes on the screen; a stack of papers and
       a pen beside it, a plain office chair. No second person, and the screen
       shows no text.
must   a person occupied at a desk with their work in front of them.
avoid  a person digging with a spade (graven); a person at a workbench
       (maken); a person asleep at the desk; a boss pointing at the door.
alt    Iemand zit achter een bureau met beide handen op het toetsenbord van
       een laptop.

vergeten   A1 irregular, rank 396
scene  one adult walks away from a small table, looking back over one
       shoulder with a slight frown; a bunch of keys on a round ring lies
       alone on the table behind them, and one hand is raised to the side of
       the head.
must   keys left behind on the table while the person walks off.
avoid  a person kneeling to look under a sofa (zoeken); the keys held up in
       triumph (vinden); a crossed-out calendar; a hand over the mouth.
alt    Iemand loopt bij een tafel vandaan en kijkt om; op de tafel blijft een
       bos sleutels liggen.

besluiten   A2 irregular, rank 2178 — SKIP
scene  SKIP: a decision has no single picturable action; a fork in the road
       would read as kiezen and a gavel as rechtspreken.
must   -
avoid  -
alt    -


BEFORE YOU FINISH
-----------------
1. The file has a header row and one row per verb, in the order of your page
   TSV.
2. awk -F'\t' 'NF!=5{print NR": "NF}' on the file prints nothing, and the
   number of lines is one more than the number of verbs on your page.
3. No two scenes on the page use the same action.
4. Every alt text is Dutch, one sentence, under 20 words, and does not contain
   the verb.
5. Every convention used is named as a convention in its scene.
