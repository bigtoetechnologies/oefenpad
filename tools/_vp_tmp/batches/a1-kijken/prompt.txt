WHAT THE STYLE IS
- Bold, clean picture-book pictogram drawing in one flat cobalt blue on pure white. Think signage or a children's picture book, drawn by hand with a confident thick marker.
- Edges are crisp and deliberate. Every stroke starts and stops cleanly. No dry-brush streaks, no smearing, no fuzzy or ragged edges, no paint texture, no smudged ground patches.
- Figures are simplified but have natural, proportionate human heads: a slightly oval head with a chin, ears, a neck and a simple hair shape, as in the three attached drawings. Not a perfect circle, not a pointed, flat or oversized head. The face stays white inside a clean cobalt outline, never filled or shaded, with a dot or short dash for each eye and a small curve or line for the mouth. No realistic anatomy, no detailed or modelled faces.
- Clothing is filled solid cobalt (jacket, trousers, dress), with thin white gaps where one part meets another (arm against body, collar, legs), so the shapes stay readable. Faces, hands and skin stay white inside a clean outline.
- Furniture and buildings (door, window, table, chair, sofa, house) are clean outlines, with the occasional small solid accent (a handle, a pot, a frame).
- No shading of any kind: no hatching, no crosshatching, no gradients, no tone, no shadows on the floor.
- Framing: always the whole figure from head to feet, seen from a few metres away, at the same distance and scale in every image. Adults have adult proportions, the head about one seventh of their height; draw a child only where the scene asks for one. Never a close-up, never a head-and-shoulders crop.
- Generous white space. The scene sits in the middle with nothing touching the edges. It must still read clearly as a small thumbnail on a phone.
- Colour: one flat cobalt, #2B50C8, nothing else: no darker or lighter blue, no second colour. The drawing is cobalt on a pure white #FFFFFF background. Never a cobalt background, never a white drawing on blue. No paper texture, no border, no shadow around the image.
- Keep it simple: few large elements, no clutter, no detailed backgrounds. Every object is drawn as a big bold shape.
- LINE QUALITY. Draw every line thick and bold, with no hairline or thin strokes and no fine detail (no spokes, stitching, tiny windows or patterns); where a real object has fine detail, simplify it into a bold shape. Every edge is perfectly smooth and clean: no jagged, stair-stepped, pixelated, speckled, noisy or fuzzy edges, and no line that breaks up, fades or doubles. Like a vector drawing: crisp at any size.

FOLLOW THE SCENE LITERALLY
- Each scene is a brief. Draw exactly what it says. Every object it names is present and instantly recognisable: a bicycle has two wheels, a frame and handlebars; a dog is a dog, not another animal or a vehicle.
- Draw the action asked for, not a nearby one: running is not panting, paying is not pouring tea.
- When two people speak to, ask or give to each other, they face each other.
- If something cannot be drawn clearly in this style, draw fewer elements rather than something unclear.
- Before you return the drawings, check each one: natural white faces with an outline (never filled), smooth bold lines with no jagged or fuzzy edges, only cobalt on white, and the action matches the scene. Redraw any that fail.

THE SCENES
kijken-door-sleutelgat.png — kijken door
Scene: Inside a room, seen side-on: a plain wall with one closed door. A person stands bent at the waist in front of it, both hands on the knees, one eye pressed close to the keyhole just below the handle and the other eye shut. The body stays wholly on this side of the door. Nothing else is in the room. The eye at the keyhole is the clearest mark.
Must read: looking through a small hole; the eye is at the opening and the body stays behind it.
Avoid: an open window with the head leaning out (that is the partner kijken uit); a key in the hand or in the lock; an ear against the door (luisteren aan); a hand on the handle (klaar voor); arms folded, standing upright before the door (wachten voor deur).

kijken-door-microscoop.png — kijken door
Scene: A person stands bent over a laboratory bench, one eye on the eyepiece of a microscope and the other eye shut, one hand on the focus knob at its side and the other steadying a small glass slide under the lens. A rack of four test tubes stands on the bench beside it. The eye on the eyepiece is the clearest mark.
Must read: looking through a microscope.
Avoid: binoculars or a magnifying glass; the person looking at the microscope from a distance (kijken naar); writing notes (werken aan verslag); a second person; markings on the tubes.

kijken-door-kier.png — kijken door
Scene: A person stands stooped against a tall fence of upright boards, both hands flat on the boards at shoulder height, one eye close to a narrow gap between two boards and the other eye shut, heels lifted slightly. The fence runs across the whole picture and stands taller than the person. The eye at the gap is the clearest mark.
Must read: looking through a crack.
Avoid: a door and keyhole (that is kijken-door-sleutelgat); the head rising over the top of the fence (kijken over); a window or parted curtains with the face in the opening (kijken uit); a low fence with two people leaning on it (praten met buur).

kijken-uit-treinraam.png — kijken uit
Scene: A cut-away side view of one train seat by a large window. A traveller sits with the body turned to the glass, elbow on the narrow ledge, chin in that hand, forehead close to the pane, the other hand resting on a bag on the lap. Beyond the glass two poles lean backward and a low line of hills is streaked with three speed lines. The streaked window is the clearest mark.
Must read: looking out of a train window during the ride.
Avoid: binoculars (kijken door); a platform and rails seen from outside (wachten op trein); a foot on the step (stappen uit); a book or phone in the hand; eyes shut (slapen).

kijken-uit-balkon.png — kijken uit
Scene: A sloping tiled roof seen from outside, at the usual distance, with one roof window swung open on its hinge. A person's head and shoulders rise through the opening, both forearms resting on the lower frame, face turned to look out across two more roof lines and a chimney beyond. The head in the open roof window is the clearest mark.
Must read: looking out of a roof window.
Avoid: a balcony or railing (that is vanaf); a person indoors at an upright window (that is kijken-uit-raam); climbing out onto the tiles; waving (zwaaien naar); a closed roof window with a face behind the glass (reads as kijken door).
