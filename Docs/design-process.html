How the home page was designed

Which designs were rejected and why, so they are not re-proposed.

A record of the 4 November 2026 design session: what was tried, what was rejected and why, and what was chosen. The rules of the chosen direction are in designguide.html; this file is the reasoning behind them, so a later session does not re-run the argument.

The fifteen prototypes are preserved in design-options/. Open design-options/index.html to see them side by side; each is a standalone page with no dependency on the repo.

What was being decided

A home page for a site whose users mostly cannot read English and are not browsing for entertainment. The constraints came in from decisions.html and did not change:

Constraint Effect on the design
Interface is Dutch No English chrome; an EN layer may exist but stays secondary
Meaning travels in pictures and colour No explanatory sentence can be load-bearing
No text inside artwork One art set serves every learner
Orange means act now, nothing else A single orange per screen
Must survive at 16px and on a phone Logo and layout both

A sixth constraint arrived during the session: the design must not look dated. Stated plainly at 14:35 — the Delftware direction "has grown off me" — and confirmed when nostalgia filters, card grids and serif display faces were all ruled out.

The rounds

Three rounds, narrowing each time. The first round tested whole visual languages; the second and third tested one idea, the logo's brush stroke, against itself.

Round Options Question being asked
1 A–E Which visual language, Delft or something else?
2 F, G What does the brush stroke do when it fills a shape?
3 H–N What does it do as an outline, and what else could the page be?

Round 1 ended with A, D and E marked naslag — kept as reference, not in contention. B and C survived. Round 2 repainted both: F is B painted, G is C painted. Round 3 flipped the brush from fill to outline (H, I), crossed the survivors (J, K), and then questioned the page itself (L, M, N).

The fifteen options

Round 1 — five visual languages (A–E)
Name What it was Verdict
A Delft, structureel Cobalt half-page carrying a hugely enlarged tile motif; games as a ruled list with diamond markers. No tiles, no cards. Reference only. The literal Delft reading is the dated one.
B Schoolplaat, nu One flat full-width canal scene — houses, bridge, boat, bikes — with words hanging as labelled leader lines. Thick ink outline, flat colour, no nostalgia filter. Survived round 1. Judged childish on second look.
C Nederlands modernisme Visible 12-column grid, heavy grotesk, primary colour blocks. Games as modules of unequal size. Survived round 1, became the chosen lineage.
D Logboek Graph paper, highlighter, handwritten marginalia, a checklist per day. The page as your own exercise book. Reference only. Handwriting and highlighter are the nostalgia the brief rejects.
E Penseel The logo's stroke becomes the whole language: the path is one long painted haul, games sit on it as nodes. No tiles, no frames. Reference only, but the brush idea it introduced drove rounds 2 and 3.

Palettes diverged widely in this round. B ran on brick, ochre, moss and sand; C on De Stijl red, yellow and blue; D on marker yellow and graphite. Only A and E stayed near a cobalt. From F onward every option shares one palette.

Rounds 2 and 3 — the brush family (F–N)
Name What it was Verdict
F Penseelplaat B repainted: the same canal scene, now solid cobalt brush fills with bristle texture instead of flat shapes with thick outlines. Strong, not chosen. Scene-led pages put the games below the fold.
G Penseelraster C repainted: the same rigid grid and heavy type, the colour blocks now painted fills with visible brush grain. Chosen.
H Penseelkader G with the fills removed: every tile is a frame of four tapering strokes that just miss at the corners, echoing the logo's border. White paper, gaps between tiles. Not chosen. Supplied the frame vocabulary that J, K, L and N reuse.
I Penseellijn F as contour drawing: the same canal in cobalt brush outlines on white, one orange door. Not chosen.
J Gracht + kader Hybrid — I's scene on top teaching five words, H's frames below as navigation. Not chosen. Parked idea, see below.
K Gemeente J with the postcard replaced by a real errand: a council counter, and labels showing a pattern rather than single words (wachten → de wachtrij). Not chosen. Parked idea, see below.
L Patroon H with the picture removed and a pattern band in its place: werkwoord + -ing = de …, with four worked examples. Not chosen. Parked idea, see below.
M Hulptaal L plus a selectable helper language (EN, УКР, PL, AR, or off) glossing words in small grey type beneath the Dutch. Not chosen. Parked idea, see below.
N Meteen beginnen No shop window: the page is the first exercise. A drawing, empty letter boxes, letters to pick, and the other games as small framed links at the side. Not chosen. Parked idea, see below.

Where the brush family splits

This is the distinction the whole second half of the session turned on. Every option below uses the logo's stroke; they differ in what the stroke is doing.

Option The brush is…
E the path — one long stroke carrying the games as nodes
F a filled shape inside a picture
G a filled tile inside a grid
H the outline of an empty tile
I the outline of a picture
J, K both — outlined picture above, outlined tiles below
L, M the outline, with the picture replaced by a grammar pattern
N the outline, applied to live exercise controls

Lineage: B → F → I, and C → G → H → L → M, with J and K crossing I against H, and N taking H's frames into the exercise itself.

Decisions

# Decision When Why
1 Drop the Delftware direction 14:35 It read as dated. The brush stroke from the logo survives; the blue-and-white tile reference does not
2 A, D, E out, kept as reference 15:15 A and D were the two most literal; E was elegant but gave the games no structure
3 B is too childish to lead 15:15 Second-party reaction. The flat scene with labels reads as a children's book, which misprices a product for adults doing inburgering
4 Try the brush as outline, not only as fill 15:21 Explicit request, and new options rather than edits, so the history stays
5 Keep every option as a file 15:43 Nothing is overwritten. This is why design-options/ has fifteen pages
6 Mobile layouts for everything still in contention 16:04 A phone layout is part of the comparison, not a later chore
7 Option G, rolled out to all pages and all games 16:25 The grid gives every game equal, scannable weight; the paint supplies warmth without a picture to decode
8 Drop C's primary red and yellow with 7 De Stijl primaries fight the orange-means-act-now rule. G keeps cobalt, deep cobalt and orange only
9 Bilingual home page, NL default 18:40 English browser gets English, everything else gets Dutch. Verified against en-GB, nl-NL, and with JS off
10 ~~The Geheugen tile becomes light cobalt~~ — tried and reverted 6 Nov Done to remove a second orange, on a too-literal reading of the orange rule. Three blues killed the page's only accent. The meter bar and the tile it points at are the same signal, so both may be orange; the guide now says so
11 Restore the brushstroke fills 6 Nov The shipped page had been painting flat rectangles since the rollout. Two causes, both now fixed and documented in designguide.html: the SVG used the tile's pixel size as its viewBox, putting the bristle texture below one pixel, and the opaque CSS fallback background filled in the gaps of a deliberately semi-transparent paint layer. The week meter bars are painted too, as in the prototype
12 Option A for the game chrome 7 Nov Six prototypes were rendered in design-options/game-chrome/: A (home nav verbatim), B (compact nav), C (one bar), D (return strip only), E (home hero at full scale), F (game in the page grid with a facts rail). A won: the home .balk verbatim, the game title in a full-width .spel-kop outside the 720 px column, the unchanged game, and the footer
13 No .taal switch on the game nav with 12 The five games that existed then were Dutch-only (the old src/shared/i18n.js pinned the language to Dutch; that file is gone and the API now returns Dutch-only strings), so an NL/EN button would visibly do nothing. The game .balk differs from the home one in that single control, which is also what storymachine already did. Making the games bilingual is a separate job, not part of adding the chrome

Why G and not F

Both were painted versions of surviving round-1 options, so the choice was between a picture-led page and a grid-led page.

The grid shows all the games above the fold and ranks them by tile size, which is how a returning learner finds where they stopped. A scene teaches five words well but pushes navigation down the page, and every new scene is new artwork. The grid needs no artwork at all.

Ideas parked, not rejected

These lost as home-page layouts. Their content ideas are better than the layouts that carried them, and none of them is recorded elsewhere.

From The idea Why it may still be worth doing
K Labels teach a pattern, not a word — aanvragen → de aanvraag Ties the site to the book's method. Five patterns beat five nouns
K The scene is a real errand (council counter) rather than a postcard canal Matches what an inburgering learner actually has to survive
L A "pattern of today" band with four worked examples and the honest caveat "Klopt niet altijd. Wel vaak genoeg om te durven gokken." A daily grammar hook that needs no artwork
M Optional helper-language gloss, learner-selected, Dutch stays primary Directly contradicts the current "no other languages" rule in decisions.html. Worth re-arguing, not worth doing by accident
N The home page is the first exercise Removes the shop window for a returning user. Could be the signed-in home page later
J A scene that teaches five words, sitting above the navigation Could be a section of the home page rather than a replacement for it

What shipped

Option G, applied to the whole site rather than the home page alone. All eight pages load assets/site/oefenpad.css and assets/site/oefenpad.js: the home page, the two legal pages, and all five games that existed at the time.

Later state (2026-11-09): there are now six games, and they are React pages served from /spelen/<game> by the .NET host rather than static pages. The five old game scripts described below no longer exist. Docs/games-backend.html describes the current arrangement.

The legal pages themselves were adapted from bigtoegames with the names changed, which is why privacy.html and voorwaarden.html predate the redesign in content and follow it in styling.

Two colours from the old guide are now wrong wherever they still appear: the cobalt #1B3A6B and the warm white #FBF8F2. The live palette is #2B50C8 on #FFFFFF. Four files still carried the retired values after the redesign; all four were corrected on 6 November 2026:

File What it was Fix
Docs/prepositions-art.md (now Docs/voorzetselpaden/a0-a2.html) Art-generation prompts, including the master prompt Repointed at the live palette. Anything generated before this arrived in the wrong blue on the wrong white
src/shared/common.js:165 (now client/src/shared/confetti.ts) Default confetti colours — the only live code affected Now cobalt, deep cobalt, light cobalt and the two oranges. The old warm white is dropped: white confetti on a white page is invisible
Docs/reuse-bigtoegames.html Told a future session to use the old palette Hexes updated
Docs/identity/ The 31 October identity sheet and three SVG marks, all in the old palette and referenced by nothing Moved to archive/identity-2026-10-31/ rather than recoloured — it is a record of a superseded identity, not a live asset
