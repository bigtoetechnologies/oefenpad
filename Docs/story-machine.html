Story Machine — design and art spec

Design and art spec for the slot-machine speaking prompt.

Docs/story-machine.html

On this page
  • Why a machine and not dice
  • The machine never writes the story
  • The word cards
  • The art
  • The words
  • What is not built

A slot machine that deals a sentence. The player picks 2 to 6 reels, pulls the handle, and reads the result aloud. The React page is client/src/games/storymachine/; the reels and their words are in server/Oefenpad.Resources/Games/Data/reels.json, and the drawing and holding rules are in server/Oefenpad.Services/Games/StoryMachineService.cs.

Why a machine and not dice

Reels are positional and dice are not. Reel 1 is always WHO, reel 2 is always DOES WHAT, so every pull lands as a sentence skeleton instead of a handful of loose nouns. That single property is what turns a toy into a language exercise, and it is why the reel order in reels.json is part of the design rather than a list.

Reel Slot Added at
1 Who 2 reels
2 Does what 2 reels
3 Where 3 reels
4 With what 4 reels
5 But then 5 reels
6 And in the end (wildcard) 6 reels

Reel 6 draws an ending most of the time and anything at all the rest, so the last beat can still surprise. It never repeats a tile already on screen.

The machine never writes the story

There is no sentence on screen. The pictures are the prompt, and spelling the result out does the player's job for them. The only on-screen text is a screen-reader-only announcement listing the tiles.

The word cards

Under each reel sits a face-down card. Turning it over shows that tile's word in the chosen language, so vocabulary is something the player asks for rather than something the machine hands them. A pull turns every card back over.

The back is the BigToe Games mascot card, taken from bigtoegames/scratchassets/playingcards/card_square_{light,dark}.jpg and scaled to 420px square at art/cardback_{light,dark}.jpg. There is no landscape back in that repo, so the cards are square — the existing backs are either square (1408px) or portrait (240x336, Solitaire). Replace the two files to rebrand; nothing else refers to them.

Reels 4 to 6 carry a join (met, maar dan, en uiteindelijk) for a read-aloud or print view later. Nothing renders it today.

The art

72 tiles, 12 per reel. The full list, with ids and both languages, is server/Oefenpad.Resources/Games/Data/reels.json; that file is the spec a generator should read. Today each tile's art is an emoji placeholder. Replacing it with a path is the only change the game needs.

The drawing style, prompt and checklist are in game-art.html. This list says what each tile must show; that file says how it is drawn.

Rule Why
No text baked into the image, ever One art set serves every language. Words are drawn in HTML, per locale
One idea per tile, readable as a silhouette at 64px A phone reel tile is about that size
Vague beats specific — "a door", not "a red door with a letterbox" The player supplies the story. Detail does it for them
Brush-drawn in cobalt, bold and sparse game-art.html; reels are read while moving
No culture-specific symbols The audience is not Dutch

Proposed layout: assets/storymachine/art/<reel-id>/<tile-id>.svg, so art/who/fox.svg. Tile ids are stable; treat them as filenames.

The words

Every tile word must be one word that a picture can show. A player turns a card to check the word against the tile's image, so a word the image cannot carry teaches nothing.

Rule Why
One word per tile — a noun or a verb A phrase has no single picture. de tijd raakt op cannot be drawn; de klok can
No prepositions, anywhere in het bos makes the tile teach in, which no image shows. The reel's slot already supplies the sense
Nouns carry de or het, never een The article is the gender, and the gender is the thing worth learning. een sleutel teaches nothing
No reflexive pronouns, adverbs or participles verstopt zich, weer thuis, wegzwevend — none of them is one drawable idea
Verbs are infinitives, both languages to run / rennen. The tile names an action; the player conjugates it aloud

The sentence the reels suggest is therefore a skeleton the player speaks aloud, not one the machine writes. The prepositions and joins live in the speaking, which is where the exercise is.

What is not built

  • No scoring, no timer, no progress. It is a prompt, not a test.
  • No audio.
  • No print view. A classroom wants one — six tiles in a row on A4.
  • No sentence display, by design.
  • No Quick Wins card back. The BigToe mascot is a placeholder.
  • Real art. Everything above runs on emoji.
