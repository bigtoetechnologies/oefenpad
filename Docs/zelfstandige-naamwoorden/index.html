How the cards work

Nouns are taught in contrast sets: two or three look-alike or often-confused words (kat/hond, stoel/bank, het bos/de bos). Every drawing in a set is the same picture with one visible thing changed, so the learner sees the pair, not the word.

A card carries the noun with its article (de/het), its plural, a diminutive flag where the headword is a diminutive (het kopje), an English gloss, the scene brief, the one mark that must read, the near misses, the contrast note, an example sentence and the alt text. Each set carries a fixed frame and one Dutch sentence that uses every member with its own article.

This is a pilot

Nouns are the largest class (about 6,300 in the lexicon). This pass takes only concrete, drawable nouns: no abstract nouns, no names, no nouns whose picture would need text. It is meant to test the card shape with article and plural before the class is done in full.

Where article and plural come from

The lexicon has no article or plural column (woorden.tsv holds nl, en, niveau, top2000, herkomst only). Every article and plural here was written from certain knowledge and is not verified by the lexicon. A native check of the list in tools/_zn_tmp/clusters.txt is the open step. Words whose article or plural is uncertain (hout, haar) were left out.

Set types

  • Look-alike nouns (kat/hond, jas/trui, mes/vork/lepel): the drawings share a frame and differ in one feature.
  • Same word, different gender: het bos (forest) and de bos (bunch), het pad (path) and de pad (toad). Both senses are real Dutch.
  • Same word, same article: de kraan (tap, crane; A2) and de slang (snake, hose; B1). The article and plural do not change; only the thing does.
  • Singular and plural (ids ending -een and -meer). Irregular plurals: kind/kinderen, ei/eieren, blad/bladeren, glas/glazen, schip/schepen. Regular plurals that change spelling: huis/huizen, muis/muizen, been/benen, boom/bomen; these are labelled "one or more", not "irregular". The drawing differs only in number, and the single card reuses the drawing of the noun's own card where one exists (been is the exception: the arm/been card must show the whole figure, so been-een and been-meer draw the bare leg). The plural card carries the article de, as every Dutch plural does. het rund (B2) is drawn as the A1 koe card without its udder, so the learner meets one animal drawing; the gloss names the single animal (cow, bull or ox), not the counting term.
  • Part sets (A2, B1, B2): one drawing with a small ring on the part named, as in the A1 face set: arm and leg joints, bird, boat, mouth, face area (the A1 head reused), fingers, tree, shirt.
  • Theme sets: 78 of the 110 look-alike and part sets share one article (kat/hond, both de), so they teach the word and not a de/het contrast, and a few (vogel/vis, sleutel/slot, zon/maan/ster) are themes rather than look-alikes. That is accepted: the set is a picture contrast, and the article is shown on every card.

How a set was placed

By default a set sits in the band of its most frequent member (rank in tools/lexicon/woorden-frequentie.tsv: A0 up to 250, A1 251–1000, A2 1001–2500, B1 2501–5000, B2 5001–10000). The rank is always the singular lemma's; plural cards (kinderen, runderen) take their singular's rank. Rank is a tiebreak only for concrete nouns: newspapers rarely mention shoes or cows, but a beginner needs them. Each reason is rendered from the lexicon by select.py and re-checked by the checker against the files, so a wrong fact fails the checker. Lexicon gives every member's niveau, top2000 and herkomst (tools/lexicon/woorden.tsv) and its rank. niveau A1 means taught in Nederlands in gang, B1 taught in Nederlands in actie, open means in Bijlage 8 but taught in neither coursebook. herkomst bijlage8 means the word is in Bijlage 8, the 5,000 most frequent lemmas (Tiberius & Schoonheim 2014): a frequency list, not a teaching list. les:taalcompleet is the TaalCompleet B1 theme vocabulary.

Basis is coursebook only when a named member is taught at or below the target band (A bands: A1 or les:nig; B1: A1 or B1 or les:nig or les:register or les:taalcompleet); every other override says judgment, not lexicon data, and its note gives the judgment. Coursebook: 9 overrides; judgment: 25. The checker also fails any B1 or B2 set holding a coursebook A1 or top-2000 word, unless the card draws another sense (wortel: the root, not the carrot).

A set takes the band of its most frequent member, so a set can hold rarer words: 16 members of B1 sets rank in B2 on their own.

The lexicon's suffixed rows ("arm (noun)", "kussen (noun)") carry a placeholder rank from a failed lookup (about 9,600 and up), so they are never used. A noun with no usable bare rank is listed in NO_RANK in select.py and banded by its partners alone:

Some ranks are shared with a homograph: knoop (button, knot), kam (comb, crest), stam (trunk, tribe), kas (greenhouse, cash), schuur (shed, the verb schuren), pols (wrist, pulse), ezel (donkey, easel), pony (pony, fringe), jaguar (the animal, the car brand), pink (little finger, the English word), zeil and schep (nouns, and forms of the verbs zeilen and scheppen), mot (moth, a quarrel) and kruk (stool, crutch or door handle). None changes a band: a partner ranks in the same band, an override places the set, or the drawn sense is the everyday one (knoop, kam; inference, not measured). de kraan and de slang draw both senses, so the shared rank is the right one.

Objects drawn in another family too

No noun card repeats another family's item, but these objects also appear there, and the illustrator should reuse one drawing of each: eend and vijver (adjectives bevroren/ontdooid, werkwoorden voeren), kruk (adjectives stevig/wankel: four legs, as here), fauteuil (comfortabel), ezel (koppig/gehoorzaam), slak (langzaam), citroen (zuur, werkwoorden persen), rits (scheidbare dichtritsen), kraan (opendraaien), vergiet (afgieten), stopcontact and kabel (aansluiten), anker (vaste uitdrukkingen voor anker), sok (op sokken), zaag (zagen), bezem (vegen), tandenborstel (poetsen), kwast and penseel (schilderen), walnoot (kraken). handdoek/laken was moved off the washing line, which belongs to the adjectives (droog, winderig/windstil).

Left out on purpose

  • Abstract nouns (idee, probleem, tijd): no honest single picture.
  • Names and brands, and nouns that need text in the picture (brief, kaart, a signboard).
  • Words with an irregular article or plural the author could not vouch for (haar, hout).
  • stad/steden as a plural set: three skylines read as one city with parks. schip/schepen replaced it (review 2026-11-20).
  • B1/B2 candidates whose article the author could not vouch for: luipaard and ree (de and het both in use), matras, duin and accordeon (de and het both in use).
  • B1/B2 candidates whose band is not certain: kussen (pillow), whose bare rank 2300 is shared with the verb kussen and would put a set in A2; vat (barrel), whose rank 2690 is shared with the verb form vat (vatten); gans, which the lexicon holds only as the adjective gans (whole).
  • B1/B2 candidates that would repeat an earlier card: mok (the same object as the A2 beaker card), handtas (the A2 bag card), kalkoen and haan (no partner in band).
  • Souvenir imagery: tulp, klomp and gracht are in the B2 lexicon and are left out under the project rule.
  • rots/kiezel (B1, dropped 2026-11-20): the contrast was size only, and kiezel (rank 8985) was drawn while commoner pairs were held back. Its place went to the fly and microwave sets.
  • Drawable B1 pairs held back by the cap of about 40 sets per band: duif/valk, rat/mol, spin/mier, perron/halte, stoep/fietspad, microfoon/koptelefoon, wolk/regenboog.

Candidates for a later pass: a same-word set for the root and the carrot (the carrot is the coursebook A1 sense), and pan (rank 2128) as an A2 card of its own.
