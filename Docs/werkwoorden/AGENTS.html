Werkwoorden — working notes for agents

One card per verb with a picture brief; irregular verbs also carry the past and perfect forms. How the pages were built, and how to change them.

The band pages are the master

The ten band pages hold every verb, one card each:

a0-irregular.html, a0-regular.html, a1-irregular.html, a1-regular.html, a2-irregular.html, a2-regular.html, b1-irregular.html, b1-regular.html, b2-irregular.html, b2-regular.html

Since 20 November 2026 the sources in tools/_ww_tmp/ are the master and the pages are rebuilt from them. Edit select.py and scenes/<page>.tsv, not the HTML.

The pages were generated once, on 19 November 2026, by tools/build-verb-cards.py from tools/_ww_tmp/selection.json and the scene notes in tools/_ww_tmp/scenes/. From then on the HTML was hand-edited, as the Voorzetselpaden band pages are. Rerunning the script overwrites all ten pages.

On 20 November 2026 the eight pages of the time were checked byte for byte against the builder's output (identical, so nobody had hand-edited them) and then rebuilt from the sources to apply the review fixes; the same day the B1/B2 pass added the two B2 pages and 83 cards from the sources. Sources and pages agree as of that date. Before the next rebuild, run the builder with --out to a scratch folder and diff it against the pages; rebuild only if they match.

index.html, matrix.html and backlog.html are generated output. Do not hand-edit them; change the source and rebuild.

A verb game ships from these pages. tools/import-verb-game.py reads only cards that carry class="beeld", groups the drawn verbs into levels of four or five, and writes verbs.json. The game shows one picture per verb, its infinitive and gloss, and the past/perfect for an irregular verb and one example sentence with its English translation (on the card, decided 26 November 2026), then asks which picture belongs to the verb. There is no conjugation drill: the forms are vocabulary to recognise. A verb with two senses that need two pictures has two cards, each in the band where its own sense is most common (missen: bus, target, person); the game never uses two cards of the same verb in one level. Bands since 26 November 2026 follow tools/_ww_tmp/bands.tsv (lexicon level, then rank, then how concrete the action is), not the rank alone; a card keeps its slug and its drawing when its band changes.

The card

One card per verb, in the base form. Each card carries one example sentence with an English translation (the example_nl and example_en columns of the scene file, decided 26 November 2026). There is deliberately no Build and no Check question: the card teaches what the verb means.

Contrast is optional (decided 20 November 2026). A contrast note appears only where a second verb shares the drawing and differs in one visible point, and the pair is written both ways in tools/_ww_tmp/contrasts.tsv. A partner may sit in another band or on another page: a verb is banded by its own frequency, so walk and run can pair across A1 and A2, while crawl keeps no partner until one exists at its level. Most verbs have no partner, and an empty field is honest; the checker fails a one-way link and reports the covered pairs.

Forms follow predictability (decided 20 November 2026). A form a learner cannot predict is vocabulary, like de or het on a noun, so it goes on the card. A form that follows a rule is grammar, taught once elsewhere. Irregular cards therefore carry the past singular and the perfect (auxiliary plus participle), for example liep and heeft/is gelopen. Regular cards carry the base form only. Other persons, the plural and the other tenses stay out. Source: Bijlage 3b, via tools/lexicon/werkwoorden-vormen.tsv.

The metadata sits in the flags and the footer: data-regularity, the frequency rank, the lexicon domain, the band, and the frame number.

Abstract verbs. A drawing cannot show "to be able to". For core A0 and A1 verbs only, a conventional pictogram is allowed — a thought bubble, a lightbulb, a balance, a question mark, a flexed arm, a weight on the back — and the scene must say in words that this is a convention, so a reviewer can see the card is not a literal picture. Above A1 an abstract verb gets no picture: it goes on the backlog.

Conventions

  • A SKIP reason is about the picture. "Two senses" is not a reason: pick the drawable sense and card it (dekken is de tafel dekken). "Another family drew it" holds only when that card draws the same scene with the same clearest mark (breien); Werkwoorden outranks every other family, so otherwise the verb is carded and the other family redraws. The checker warns on "two senses" and fails a cited family:id that does not exist.
  • One card, one verb, one drawing. A second sense does not get a second card in this pass. vinden is to find, not to think; scheppen is to scoop, not to create, so it is regular.
  • A card without a picture stays on the page. Never delete the markup. It carries div.geenbeeld and data-status="proposal"; when the drawing arrives, put the PNG in assets/verbs/<slug>.png and rerun build-verb-cards.py. A card counts as drawn when that file exists: the builder swaps the div for <img class="beeld">, sets data-status="drawn" and updates the counts. Draw with tools/grok/grok.py verbprompt <page> --batch N (seven cards a batch), then review every image against its Scene and Must read before copying it in. All 90 A0 and A1 cards were drawn on 20 November 2026. Weak spots: krijgen follows its brief but reads close to geven, and kiezen shows an open hand beside two jars, not a hand above one. When a drawn card's brief changes, list its slug in tools/_ww_tmp/redraw.tsv: the card then shows opnieuw tekenen and data-status="redraw" until the new PNG replaces the old one. hebben and zien are listed; doen.png and laten.png belong to cards that became SKIP on 20 November.
  • Alt text never names the verb, and never uses a form of it, nor a noun built on its stem (zwemmer, weegschaal). It describes the picture. That keeps it honest for a screen reader and stops it leaking the answer in a game. Nor does it use a word on another root of the verb (gras for grazen, druppel for druipen; listed in COGNATES), or a form of a verb its own Avoid names as the competitor (giet on the mengen card). check-verb-cards.py tests all of this.
  • The artwork is one cobalt line drawing. Scene, Must and Avoid name no colour (red, gold, grey), and Scene and Must ask for no text, digits, numbers or musical notes, no souvenir imagery (cheese, tulips) and no religious marker. Greeked strokes standing in for writing are not text (decided 20 November 2026), so a signature can be drawn as a scribble. The checker tests these too, and that no two cards share an English gloss.
  • data-id is the slug, and it equals the <slug>.png in the footer and the id on the article. Rename all three together.
  • The frame number runs on across the ten pages: A0 irregular first, then A0 regular, and so on to B2 regular. Within a page the cards are in frequency order. Adding a card renumbers everything after it; rerun the builder or renumber by hand.
  • The level is the lexicon's frequency rank, not the niveau column in woorden.tsv. That column is the coursebook tier (A1, B1, open) and has nothing to do with CEFR.
  • Regular and irregular never share a page. A learner meets zijn before werken, and the two pages are read in that order.

Tools

python3 tools/_ww_tmp/select.py       # selection.json, the ten page TSVs, backlog_seed.tsv
python3 tools/build-verb-forms.py     # tools/lexicon/werkwoorden-vormen.tsv, from Bijlage 3b (carded verbs only; appends, never changes a row; --dry-run)
python3 tools/build-verb-cards.py     # the ten band pages, index.html, matrix.html, backlog.html
python3 tools/check-verb-cards.py     # asserts ids, fields, frames, counts, anchors, and the content rules above

select.py reads the lexicon TSVs in tools/lexicon/ and the two appendices in /media/tony/Data/Dutch (Bijlage 3b, irregular verbs; Bijlage 4, separable verbs). It is the only place a verb's band, regularity or rank is decided. A verb is irregular when Bijlage 3b lists it, when it is a prefix on a listed base (vervangen, bezoeken, beschrijven), or when it is in EXTRA_IRREGULAR (strong verbs the appendix lacks: meten, heffen, laden, wringen). A verb is separable when Bijlage 4 or the hand list says so, or when it starts with an unambiguous separable particle on a known verb (nadenken, ophouden). Separable verbs belong to the Scheidbare werkwoorden family, so aanvallen, loslaten and overnemen left these pages on 20 November; their scene rows are kept in tools/_ww_tmp/scenes/retired.tsv.

build-verb-forms.py reads Bijlage 3b and writes one row per irregular verb: past singular, past plural, auxiliary (hebben, zijn or hebben/zijn where the appendix marks it optional) and participle. Rows the appendix cannot supply cleanly (willen, zullen, the EXTRA_IRREGULAR verbs) are in its MANUAL table; prefixed verbs take the base verb's forms (vervangen: verving, heeft vervangen), with the auxiliary corrected in AUX where it differs (vertrekken: is vertrokken). Where the appendix is wrong for the carded sense, CORRECT overrides it: wijken takes zijn, springen, scheiden and smelten take hebben/zijn; schuilen shows schuilde / school, heeft geschuild / gescholen (weak forms first, for the shelter sense) and waaien waaide / woei. The appendix prints those second forms on a continuation line the parser skips. PARTIAL holds appendix rows that lack a plural (rijgen). The plural is stored but not shown. The script writes rows only for carded verbs: an irregular verb whose scene row is SKIP gets none, because its forms were never reviewed (the old script would have added 56 such rows, some with the wrong auxiliary: beklimmen got hebben/zijn). It never changes or drops an existing row, since other families append rows and some were corrected by hand; it only appends carded verbs missing from the TSV, and reports kept rows that differ from the appendix. Run it with --dry-run first: it prints the rows it would add and writes nothing. Other agents append to this TSV too, so add rows only for verbs missing from it, and re-read it just before editing. Once a page is hand-edited, correct a form on the page and in the TSV, or a rebuild reverts it.

build-verb-cards.py refuses to run if a page has no scene row for one of its verbs, so a missing row fails loudly instead of producing a blank card. A scene row that begins with SKIP: drops the verb from the page and lists it on the backlog with the reason. Frame numbers are assigned only to the cards that are actually drawn, so the ten pages run #1 to #300 with no gaps.

How a verb was chosen

Bands are cut by frequency rank from tools/lexicon/woorden-frequentie.tsv: A0 up to 250, A1 251–1000, A2 1001–2500, B1 2501–5000, B2 5001–10000. Above 10,000 the lexicon has no rank.

A0 and A1 keep every usable verb. A2 keeps every irregular plus the top sixty regulars, because the regular tail is long and that page had to finish. B1 and B2 have no cap since 20 November 2026: every usable verb is on its page, as a card or as a SKIP row with a reason. Drawable B1/B2 verbs not carded in that pass are in LATER in select.py and go to the backlog with a note. The B1/B2 pass had a budget of up to 120 cards and added 83 (27 in B1, 56 in B2); the review fix pass the same day added 11 more (zwaaien, strijken, schommelen, dekken, ondertekenen, regenen, lekken, schoppen, roeren, gieten, wenken) and moved schaatsen back to B1; a third pass replaced the last 25 "two senses" reasons, carding ruimen (snow), schakelen (gear), pikken (peck), isoleren (insulate) and stoken (a stove) and giving the other twenty a reason about the picture (zweren yields to the Voorzetselpaden oath cards); the rest of the tail is abstract, administrative, a screen action, a kind of speaking, or the picture of a card that already exists. "Every irregular" includes the prefixed ones since the 20 November review, so most of the new ones are SKIP rows: abstract verbs such as verwijzen or overwinnen whose only picture is another card.

1034 verbs were selected. 300 got a card; 734 were written up as SKIP because no honest single drawing carries them — abstract or idiom-only meanings, or the same action as a neighbour. These are the skipped verbs. A further 603, the held-back verbs, were set aside before selection and never reached a band page, so the two counts never overlap: 460 separable, 32 reflexive, 20 sensitive, 14 odd lemmas, 73 A2 regulars below the cut, 2 drawable verbs left for a later pass (bedekken, schuren), and 2 both separable and reflexive. Every one is on backlog.html with its reason, so nothing is lost by not having a card yet.

Pitfalls

  • The lexicon writes some verbs with a parenthetical, zijn (verb), eten (verb), kosten (verb). Strip it before a lookup or the verb silently disappears.
  • Those same senses come back from wordfreq with a nonsense rank (zipf 2.38, rank ~9000). select.py overrides seven by hand: zijn 1, eten 250, kosten 450, leven 550, bestaan 1400, kussen 1800, optreden 3000.
  • The opposite problem: a verb spelled like a common noun plural (boeken, landen, plaatsen, stemmen, wijken, golven) gets the noun's frequency and lands a band or two too early. rank_of moves twelve of them one band later by hand (the B1/B2 pass added remmen and hakken; schaatsen was reverted because the singular schaats is rarer than the verb), each with a reason in RANK_REASON that the overview prints. That is judgement, not a lemma count; a POS-tagged frequency list would settle it.
  • woorden-woordsoort.tsv carries a noun row and a verb row for the same spelling (eten, kussen, leven, bestaan, optreden). Dedupe by lemma, keep the verb row, and take the lowest rank.
  • The domain tag comes from woorden-domein.tsv, which files a lemma under its most common nominal sense. Several dozen verbs would have read absurdly — bellen as Sport & games, veranderen as Food & drink, breken as Time. Those are overridden in a DOMAIN map in select.py; add to it rather than editing the lexicon.
  • Separable detection reads Bijlage 4 with a regex and then filters the hits against the verb list. The raw parse also throws up bijlage, ijlage and van, which are not verbs; a hand-written list in select.py covers what the appendix misses.
  • The scene notes are TSV. A tab inside a field breaks the row silently, which is why the builder checks every verb against the page TSV before it writes anything.
