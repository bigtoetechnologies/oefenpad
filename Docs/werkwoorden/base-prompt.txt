Each card in Docs/werkwoorden/ is one drawing for one Dutch verb in its base form. Make one drawing per verb, in exactly the style of the Voorzetselpaden frames already in the repository (bang-voor-hond.png, zorgen-oma.png, trots-op-kind.png). Copy how they are drawn, not their subjects or composition.

WHAT THE STYLE IS
- Bold, clean picture-book pictogram drawing in one flat cobalt blue on pure white. Think signage or a children's picture book, drawn by hand with a confident thick marker.
- Edges are crisp and deliberate. Every stroke starts and stops cleanly. No dry-brush streaks, no smearing, no fuzzy or ragged edges, no paint texture, no smudged ground patches.
- Figures are simplified but have natural, proportionate human heads: a slightly oval head with a chin, ears, a neck and a simple hair shape, as in the existing drawings. Not a perfect circle, not a pointed, flat or oversized head. The face stays white inside a clean cobalt outline, never filled or shaded, with a dot or short dash for each eye and a small curve or line for the mouth. No realistic anatomy, no detailed or modelled faces.
- Clothing is filled solid cobalt (jacket, trousers, dress), with thin white gaps where one part meets another (arm against body, collar, legs), so the shapes stay readable. Faces, hands and skin stay white inside a clean outline.
- Furniture and buildings (door, window, table, chair, sofa, house, workbench) are clean outlines, with the occasional small solid accent (a handle, a pot, a frame).
- No shading of any kind: no hatching, no crosshatching, no gradients, no tone, no shadows on the floor.
- Framing: always the whole figure from head to feet, seen from a few metres away, at the same distance and scale in every image. Adults have adult proportions, the head about one seventh of their height; draw a child only where the scene asks for one. Never a close-up, never a head-and-shoulders crop.
- Generous white space. The scene sits in the middle with nothing touching the edges. It must still read clearly as a small thumbnail on a phone.
- Colour: one flat cobalt, #2B50C8, nothing else: no darker or lighter blue, no second colour. The drawing is cobalt on a pure white #FFFFFF background. Never a cobalt background, never a white drawing on blue. No paper texture, no border, no shadow around the image.
- Keep it simple: few large elements, no clutter, no detailed backgrounds. Every object is drawn as a big bold shape.
- LINE QUALITY. Draw every line thick and bold, with no hairline or thin strokes and no fine detail (no spokes, stitching, tiny windows or patterns); where a real object has fine detail, simplify it into a bold shape. Every edge is perfectly smooth and clean: no jagged, stair-stepped, pixelated, speckled, noisy or fuzzy edges, and no line that breaks up, fades or doubles. Like a vector drawing: crisp at any size.
- COMMON FAULTS TO AVOID. One single flat cobalt for everything: no navy, no lighter tint, no second tone, no hatching, no shadows or ground ellipses. No text, letters, numbers or currency symbols anywhere, not even on objects. Keep the whole drawing, including every object, at least 5% inside the frame on all sides. Draw each named object once; add no extra objects, boxes, bags or people the scene does not name. Hands are open and clearly drawn, not hidden or in pockets.

FOLLOW THE SCENE LITERALLY
- Each scene is a brief. Draw exactly what it says. Every object it names is present and instantly recognisable: a bicycle has two wheels, a frame and handlebars; a saw has teeth along one edge; a kettle has a spout and a handle.
- Draw the action asked for, not a nearby one: walking is not running, giving is not holding, paying is not pouring tea.
- A verb is a moment, not a pose. Catch the action at its clearest instant: the box halfway between two pairs of hands, the foot just leaving the ground, the nail half driven in.
- When two people give, show, ask or hand something to each other, they face each other and look at the thing between them.
- If the card's Scene says a symbol is a convention (a thought bubble, a lightbulb, a balance, a question mark), draw that symbol plainly and exactly once, and add no other symbol to the frame.
- If something cannot be drawn clearly in this style, draw fewer elements rather than something unclear.
- Before you return the drawings, check each one: natural white faces with an outline (never filled), smooth bold lines with no jagged or fuzzy edges, only cobalt on white, and the action matches the scene. Redraw any that fail.

FORMAT
Each image 784 × 1168 px, 2:3 portrait. No text, letters, numbers or punctuation anywhere in the drawings: no labels, no signs, no writing on screens, paper, boxes or clothing.
