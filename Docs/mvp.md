MVP: Minimal Effort to Test the Idea

  The goal isn't to build the ecosystem — it's to find out whether English speakers actually feel the "quick win" payoff the concept promises. Cheapest way to learn that:

  Option A — Paper Memory Deck (cheapest, ~1 weekend)

  Build: One deck only — Cognates (-tion → -tie, -ity → -iteit, -al → -aal). ~30 pairs. Home-printed, glued to card.

  Test: Sit 3–5 English-speaking friends down, play a 10-minute memory match. Watch their faces. Ask afterwards:
  - "Did Dutch feel more familiar than you expected?"
  - "Would you pay €10 for a box of these?"
  - "Would you want more decks?"

  What you learn: Whether the core emotional hook (instant recognition → confidence) actually lands. If it doesn't here, no website or app will save it.

  Cost: A few euros, one weekend.

  Option B — One-Page Web Demo (~2–3 evenings)

  Build: A single static React/Svelte page with one mini-game — drag English words onto matching Dutch cognates, or a 20-card flip-match. No backend, no accounts, no progress
  tracking. Deploy free on Netlify/Vercel.

  Test: Share link in 2–3 expat/learning-Dutch subreddits or Facebook groups. Track: do people finish it? Do they ask for more? Add a single email-capture: "Want the next 5 modules?
  Drop your email."

  What you learn: Whether strangers (not friends being polite) get hooked, and whether the concept survives contact with the actual target audience. Email signups = the real
  validation signal.

  Cost: Free hosting, your time.

  Recommended: Do Both, In Order

  1. Weekend 1 — paper deck. Cheapest gut-check. If friends shrug, rethink the concept before writing any code.
  2. Weekend 2 — web demo + Reddit post. If the deck worked, see if it scales to strangers and captures emails.

  Two weekends, ~€10, and you'll know whether to commit the next 6 months.

  Explicitly NOT in the MVP

  - No app (React Native = weeks of yak-shaving)
  - No accounts, login, or progress sync
  - No backend or .NET API
  - No book draft
  - No more than one content category (cognates only — proves the pattern, doesn't drown you in content creation)
  - No false friends, prepositions, irregular verbs — those are Level 2+

  Success Criteria

  Before starting, decide what "yes, keep going" looks like. Suggested:
  - Paper: at least 3 of 5 testers say "that was actually fun / I want more" unprompted.
  - Web: at least 20 email signups from a single Reddit post, or completion rate >60%.

  If you hit those, the UWV pitch writes itself — you'll have real evidence, not just a concept doc.
