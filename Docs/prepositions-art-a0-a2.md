do not add 4b rows here.

## How to use this file

Each numbered section is a self-contained prompt. Paste the whole fenced block
into Grok, one block at a time. Grok has no file access, so nothing outside the
block is assumed. Ask for each prompt's ten images, then process the result as
described in `game-art.md` under *Delivered raster art* and ship at 512 × 763.

The house style is the bold brush-pen sketch defined in `game-art.md`: cobalt
`#2B50C8` only, wide-nib contour with directional hatching, 2:3 portrait, one
idea per image, no text. The prompts repeat those rules because the generator
cannot read `game-art.md`.

`zonder` and `tot` are deliberately absent.

- `zonder + noun` (`zonder afspraak`, `zonder moeite`) is free prepositional use
  and just means "without + the noun", so it teaches the word `zonder`, not a
  chunk. `prepositions-roadmap.md` §11 rules this out of the frame set. Items
  14–17 therefore use real verb/adjective + preposition chunks instead: `letten
  op`, `bezig zijn met`, `geïnteresseerd zijn in`, `tevreden zijn met`.
- `tot ziens` and `tot morgen` are farewell formulae and `tot nu toe` is a fixed
  adverbial; in none of the three does a verb, adjective or noun select `tot`.
  Items 18–20 are therefore real frames with a governed preposition: `bedanken
  voor`, `feliciteren met`, `uitnodigen voor`.

## Batch 4a — images 1–10

````text
You have no file access. Do not ask to read any repository, document, image or
attachment. Everything you need is in this message.

TASK
Generate ten separate images for a Dutch practice game called Voorzetselpaden,
and nothing else. Each image is a wordless clue for one Dutch verb or adjective
plus a fixed preposition. The learner sees the image, recognises the everyday
situation, and connects it to the chunk. A Dutch speaker must be able to name
the situation from the drawing alone, without a caption. The Dutch and English
below are context for you only. None of those words may appear in the image.

STYLE — FOLLOW EXACTLY
- Bold brush-pen sketch in the hand of the Oefenpad logo: a wide-nib brush or
  chisel marker, thick pressure-varying strokes with dry-brush streaks and
  split bristles. Not pencil, fine-liner, flat vector, cartoon, stock,
  photorealistic, 3D or soft-shaded.
- Cobalt blue #2B50C8 only. No orange, red, green, second blue, grey, skin
  colour or full colour.
- Plain pure white background (#FFFFFF). No off-white, cream, beige or grey
  background. The white paper is intentional; it becomes transparency later.
  No gradients, soft shading, stippling, drop shadows, glow, blur, grain or
  paper texture. No border, frame, card, rounded rectangle or white plate.
- Contour plus hatching. Important edges are drawn twice, a few pixels off,
  and corners may stay open. Strokes taper: thinner at the ends, wider at the
  point of pressure. At 784 px wide: most lines 4–8 px, boldest contours
  9–14 px, hatching 2–4 px (at 1024 px wide: 5–10, 12–18, 3–5). Ink covers
  14–22% of the canvas, up to 27% for a scene with two figures and furniture.
- Hatching is wanted: quick, dense, directional strokes on the shadow side of
  folds and under objects, with white paper still visible between them. Never
  cross-hatch.
- Fill hair only, plus one or two small accents. Clothing, furniture and
  background stay white inside the contour. Never build the scene from filled
  silhouettes.
- One idea per image. The scene spans 90–100% of the width and the person
  35–55%; it may touch the left or right edge, with paper kept at top and
  bottom. Must still be nameable at 64 × 64 px.
- People have adult proportions, profile or three-quarter view, facing right:
  brow, one eye, nose line, mouth, ear, filled hair. Hands have a thumb and
  finger lines. No cartoon faces, open mouths or motion lines.
- Posture carries the meaning. The drawing is a clue for an adult learner,
  not a cartoon for children.
- No text, letters, numbers, words, logos, emoji, punctuation, arrows or
  readable signs anywhere. A sign in a scene must be blank. Blank speech
  bubbles are allowed as shapes, but they contain no letters, words or
  punctuation. Do not use a question mark.
- No decorative background: no plants, posters, wallpaper, shelves,
  landscapes or scenery that is not the idea.
- No culture-specific or Dutch souvenir imagery: no windmills, clogs, tulips,
  canals, cheese or similar. Everyday objects are fine.
- If the scene needs a caption to be understood, simplify or redraw it.

FORMAT
- Ten separate images, one per subject.
- 2:3 portrait, for example 1024 × 1536 px. All ten use the same camera, the
  same scale and the same line weight, because they sit side by side in one
  game.
- Raster PNG. Do not produce SVG code.
- No contact sheet, grid, collage or extra images. If you must return them one
  at a time, return them in the numbered order.
- Do not put the filename, a number or any label inside the image. Put the
  exact filename in the chat text above or below each image.

THE TEN SUBJECTS, IN ORDER

1. zeggen-tegen-persoon.png — Dutch chunk: zeggen tegen (to say to).
   Scene: two people close together; one leans in and says one short thing to
   the other, with a single small blank speech shape between them. The speaker
   is the clearest mark; the listener is calm and facing them.
   Must read: saying one short thing to someone.
   Avoid: a long story scroll (that is #2); a gift or object being handed
   over; two people arguing.

2. vertellen-aan-kind.png — Dutch chunk: vertellen aan (to tell to).
   Scene: one adult crouched or seated at eye level with a child, hands moving
   as if telling a longer story; a long blank ribbon or scroll shape rises
   between them. The child listens, chin up.
   Must read: telling a story to someone.
   Avoid: a single small speech shape (that is #1); a book with readable
   pages; a classroom or crowd.

3. praten-tegen-muur.png — Dutch chunk: praten tegen (to talk at).
   Scene: one person talking animatedly with open hands toward another person
   who has turned their back and is walking away. No speech bubble; posture
   does the work.
   Must read: talking at someone who is not listening.
   Avoid: two people facing each other and talking (that is the shipped
   praten met scene); a literal brick wall; a phone.

4. beschermen-tegen-regen.png — Dutch chunk: beschermen tegen (to protect
   against).
   Scene: one person standing dry and calm under a large open umbrella while
   rain slants down around them; the umbrella is the strongest silhouette.
   Must read: being protected from the weather.
   Avoid: a soaked person (that is #5); a second person; a sunny sky.

5. komen-door-regen.png — Dutch chunk: komen door (to be caused by).
   Scene: one soaked person, no umbrella, hair and clothes dripping, pointing
   up at a rain cloud as if naming the cause. The rain cloud and the wet
   person are the two masses.
   Must read: the rain is the cause.
   Avoid: an umbrella (that is #4); a person looking at a watch; a bus stop.

6. komen-uit-huis.png — Dutch chunk: komen uit (to come from).
   Scene: one person with a suitcase stepping out of the open door of a simple
   house, mid-stride, as if arriving from somewhere else. The doorway is the
   clearest mark.
   Must read: coming from a place.
   Avoid: a bus or car (that is stappen uit); rain; a person entering the
   house rather than leaving it.

7. kijken-door-sleutelgat.png — Dutch chunk: kijken door (to look through).
   Scene: one person bent toward a door, one eye close to a keyhole or a narrow
   gap, peering through. The door and the eye line are the main idea.
   Must read: looking through a small opening.
   Avoid: a window (that is #8); a telescope; a key in hand.

8. kijken-uit-raam.png — Dutch chunk: kijken uit (to look out of).
   Scene: one person standing at a window, hands on the sill or curtains
   pulled aside, looking out. The window frame is the clearest mark.
   Must read: looking out of a window.
   Avoid: a keyhole or door crack (that is #7); a person outside looking in;
   a landscape beyond the window.

9. doorgeven-aan-buur.png — Dutch chunk: doorgeven aan (to pass on to).
   Scene: two people on either side of a low fence or table; one hands a plain
   box or book across to the other, both hands visible. The object is plain,
   with no ribbon.
   Must read: passing something on to the next person.
   Avoid: a wrapped gift with a ribbon (that reads as blij met); a crowd; a
   telephone.

10. doorbrengen-met-vriend.png — Dutch chunk: doorbrengen met (to spend time
    with).
    Scene: two people relaxed side by side on a simple sofa; one reads a book,
    one holds a cup; a small plain wall clock without numbers. Both look
    comfortable, not busy.
    Must read: passing time together.
    Avoid: a party or celebration; a gift; a workplace; readable clock
    numbers.

CONTRAST PAIRS — DESIGN THESE TOGETHER
1 and 2 are a pair: same two figures, same camera, same distance. Change only
the speech shape — one small blob versus a long ribbon — and the listener's
posture.
4 and 5 are a pair: same rain, same camera distance. Change only the umbrella
and whether the person is dry or soaked.
7 and 8 are a pair: same person, same camera. Change only the opening — keyhole
versus window.
Draw each pair in the same sitting. At 64 × 64 px the two images must be
clearly different, and neither may suggest the other's chunk.
3, 6, 9 and 10 are singles; draw each one standalone. #3 must not repeat the
face-to-face composition of the shipped praten met scene.

VALIDATION BEFORE YOU FINISH
For every image:
- Downscale mentally or actually to 64 × 64: is the idea still nameable?
- Is it cobalt only, with no orange, no grey and no second blue?
- Is there any text, letter, number, logo, emoji, punctuation, arrow or
  readable sign? Remove it.
- Are the strokes tapered filled shapes, not uniform outlines?
- Is the background plain white, with no border, card, shadow or scenery?
- Does the scene span the frame without shrinking into a card, with the person 35–55% of the width?
Then check the pairs side by side. If either image in a pair suggests the
other's chunk, redraw it before delivering.

When finished, list the ten filenames and name any image whose 64 px
silhouette is still ambiguous. Do not add any image that is not on this list.
````

## Appendix — three extra scenes per frame (proposal, not delivered)

Added 10 November 2026. This appendix does **not** change the delivered fifty
or the status of batch 4. It proposes three further scenes for every A0–A2
frame, so each chunk has four exemplars instead of one. Nothing here is drawn,
promoted or in `prepositions.json`; every row is `planned`.

### Recommendation

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| How many new scenes? | **150**: three for each of the fifty shipped frames |
| Total A0–A2 scenes afterwards | **200** (fifty existing + 150) |
| New Grok batches | **14**, grouped by level so every contrast pair stays in one batch |
| What about batch 4b? | Not here. Its sixteen frames are still unintegrated; give them the same three extras only after they are in the game (48 more, 198 new in total) |
| Is more than four per chunk worth it? | No. Four varied exemplars is enough for A0–A2; the gain after that is small and the art cost is linear |

Why three and not one or two: one scene teaches recognition of that scene, not
the chunk. Two gives a contrast but still lets the learner memorise a single
pairing. Four different situations force the preposition to carry the meaning
across contexts. The contrast pairs benefit most, because the learner must keep
`op` and `voor`, `aan` and `over`, `met` and `tegen` apart when the setting
changes.

### Engine implication (for whoever codes this)

`prepositions.json` currently stores one `scene` string per frame. These 150
files cannot be used by adding them to `scenes` alone: the engine needs to know
which frame each variant belongs to, choose one variant per session, and never
offer two variants of the same frame as Kies options, because both would be
correct. The Klopt stop also needs the scene's frame. This is a schema and
engine change, not an art drop. Do not promote these files until that is
decided.

### How a redraw batch is phrased

Grok gets a fresh context about every ten images, so nothing survives between
sessions except what is inside the paste block. Two consequences:

- **Never attach the old drawing.** The old files are cartoon, solid-filled and
dot-eyed; attaching one in a fresh context invites a traced copy of exactly
what we are removing, and nothing in that context would tell Grok it is a
before-picture. Describe the scene in words instead.
- **Every block carries the whole brief** — header, filenames, scenes, pair
checks, validation. Nothing may be left to an earlier message.

One block is pasted with the three anchors attached: the locked header from
`game-art.md`, then one batch block. One block per fresh context, five images
(R1 is four). Two blocks can share a context, but the line weight creeps by
image ten, so five is the safe number.

The briefs below are the old ones translated into the new visual language. The
meaning and the confusions are unchanged; "delighted", "scowling", "hands
raised" and "the gift is the main mass" are gone, because they describe the
cartoon. What replaces them is posture, eye line and which mark is clearest.

`R1` is the test batch. Continue to `R2` only if all four pass beside the
anchors at full size and at 64 px.

The ten paste-ready blocks are in `prepositions-art-redraw-prompts.md`: the
locked header once, then R1–R10. Each block is self-contained apart from the
header, and the header is re-pasted after every context reset.
