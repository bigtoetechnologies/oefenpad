Where the 10,000 words come from

The selection rule and the provenance counts: what was taken, what was left, and the arithmetic that decided it.

Written 17 November 2026; every figure re-measured on 18 November 2026 after the coursebook additions. Every number below was produced by re-running the selection against the files in this directory, not copied from an earlier report. All source research — what was downloaded, what it contained, what it was licensed under — is in Docs/lexicon/aanvulling.html; this file states the selection rule and the provenance counts, and points there for the rest.

The answer in one paragraph

Nobody chose the online words by hand. The 10,000 is 7,556 words the owner already owned or curated (the coursebook appendix, the games' picture vocabulary, the TaalCompleet KNM list and the coursebook additions) plus 2,444 words taken from English Wiktionary by frequency rank: the 10,442 kaikki candidates were scored with wordfreq, sorted by descending zipf frequency (alphabetical on ties), and cut at zipf 3.35. The alphabetical tie-break at that cut-off decided 54 of the 58 words sitting exactly on the boundary, so the last few words in the file are arbitrary. 7,805 usable candidates were left unused — that is the headroom if the target ever rises.

The four steps

Step Script Adds Running total
1 extraheer-woorden.py Bijlage 8 appendix + the six games' word banks 7,107
2 knm-woorden.py TaalCompleet KNM vocabulary 7,232
3 aanvul-woorden.py curated coursebook vocabulary the frequency cut misses 7,556
4 verrijk-woorden.py kaikki candidates, ranked by wordfreq 10,000

Only step 4 involves a choice. Steps 1–3 are exhaustive: every lemma those sources supply that is not already present is taken. Step 3 is curated by hand from aanvul-woorden.tsv, but it too adds every row it is given.

The selection rule, as arithmetic

Inputs: kandidaten.tsv (10,442 rows) and woorden.tsv as step 3 left it (7,556 rows).

count
candidate rows in kandidaten.tsv 10,442
skipped because already present, blank or repeated −193
actually scored with zipf_frequency(nl, "nl") 10,249
needed to reach 10,000 2,444
sorted by (-zipf, nl) — descending frequency, alphabetical tie-break
cut-off zipf 3.3500
candidates sitting exactly at 3.35 58
of those, taken 54
unused survivors 7,805

The 193 is smaller than the 7,556 words already on the list because the candidate pool was built against an earlier state of woorden.tsv: most coursebook words are not in kandidaten.tsv at all. Whatever did overlap is skipped, not double-counted. The KNM step itself: 321 rows / 279 distinct headwords → 125 added, 152 already present or repeated, 44 phrases skipped (multi-word or containing digits), 0 empty glosses. The coursebook step added 324 of its 325 curated rows (zo’n was already recovered from the appendix).

What was left on the table

All 10,442 candidates already passed the zipf >= 2.5 gate when kandidaten.tsv was built (step 6 of the recipe in Docs/lexicon/aanvulling.html). The selection therefore threw away real, usable, glossed lemmas:

unused candidates with zipf ≥ count
2.50 7,805
3.00 2,395
3.10 1,602
3.20 916
3.25 600
3.30 282
3.35 4

That is deliberate, not an oversight: the appendix is the 5,000 most frequent Dutch lemmas, so the honest continuation of it is the next most frequent words. Nothing rarer than the cut-off was taken while a more frequent word was available. The cost is that the tail is dull rather than useful — the 2.5–3.0 band holds encyclopaedic and specialist vocabulary (zeeniveau, belastingdruk, conservatorium), and nothing here reads the "topics" tags Wiktionary carries on 5,360 senses.

If the target moved to 12,000, the next 2,000 words are already on disk and need no new source — verrijk-woorden.py --target 12000 is the whole change; at that point the cut-off is around zipf 3.0. The pool runs out at 17,805 in total (7,556 + 10,249), beyond which a new extraction from a lower gate would be needed.

The 2,444 are mostly nouns, and that matters

Part of speech of the kaikki rows:

pos rows share
noun 1,900 77.7%
adj 256 10.5%
verb 240 9.8%
adv 35 1.4%
function 8 0.3%
interjection 3 0.1%
name 2 0.1%

So "10,000 words to B2" is a noun claim more than anything. The appendix already carries the verb and adjective spine (1,297 verbs, 800 adjectives); the extension adds nouns. If the goal is B2 reading, that is defensible — most of what a B2 text adds beyond B1 is nominal — but it is not a balanced list, and no source found supplies a frequency-ranked B2 verb or adjective list to fix it.

Provenance of the 10,000

herkomst rows what it is
bijlage8 (sole origin) 4,709 the coursebook appendix
bijlage8;spel:… 453 appendix words that are also in a game's picture bank
spel:… (games only) 1,945 picture vocabulary from the six games
knm 125 TaalCompleet KNM
kaikki 2,444 frequency-ranked Wiktionary extension
les:* 321 curated coursebook vocabulary (NIG, NIA register, TaalCompleet B1 themes 1–5)
handmatig 3 confirmed high-frequency gaps
total 10,000

The 26 game banks behind the spel: tags are animals, art, cities, countries, dinosaurs, fashion, flowers, food, history, household, insects, literature, marine, mountains, movies, music, mythology, nature, oceans, professions, reels, science, space, sports, technology, travel. Many rows carry two or three tags.

Read another way:

  • 5,162 rows come from the frequency appendix (4,709 + 453).
  • 2,398 rows are in at least one game bank; 453 of those are also appendix words, so 1,945 are game-only.
  • The "general Dutch spine" — appendix, KNM, coursebook additions and Wiktionary, no picture game — is 8,055 words. The remaining 1,945 are concrete, pictureable vocabulary.

What counts as a "word" here

woorden-woordsoort.tsv gives the shape of the 10,000:

pos rows
noun 6,318
verb 1,638
adj 1,127
adv 359
name 315 countries, cities, people — proper nouns, not vocabulary
function 206 prepositions, pronouns, conjunctions, numerals
interjection 36
phrase 1

If "word a learner wants to learn" means a content word, the usable figure is 9,479 after removing the 206 function words and 315 proper names — and 1,945 of those are picture vocabulary the games already teach. The 206 function words are deliberately kept: a learner needs them, but they are taught as grammar, not as vocabulary, which is why thema files them all as algemeen/laag and the domain axis puts them in Function words.

Note what the appendix is: it is a tiered list. Light blue page fill means the word is taught in Nederlands in gang (A1), orange means Nederlands in actie (B1), unmarked means it is in the 5,000 most frequent but in neither coursebook — which woorden.tsv records as niveau = open (3,468 rows). There is no B2 tier in that source and no B2 label anywhere in these files.

No public B2 word list exists. CEFR publishes none; the Groene Boekje is spelling only; SUBTLEX-NL and Routledge are restricted; NT2Lex is CC BY-NC-SA. 8,000–10,000 lemmas is the usual estimate for B2 receptive vocabulary — see Docs/lexicon/index.html for the citations. It is a target, not a measurement, and the 10,000 here is a frequency-ordered approximation of it, not a validated B2 list.

What the coursebooks added, and what they did not

Comparing our list against the three coursebook extracts was a coverage check, not a copy job. Every headword was normalised (lowercase, straight apostrophe, leading article and zich stripped), deduplicated, and tested against the 10,000 before anything was added. The result:

Source Headwords Gaps in our list Added
Nederlands in gang 1,013 117 93
TaalCompleet B1, themes 1–5 618 84 68
Nederlands in actie register 1,080 215 165

The union of the three gap sets was 410 distinct lemmas. Of those, 266 were already curated, and 59 more cleared the selection rule (zipf, plural-lemma test, no transparent inflections or proper nouns), giving the 325 rows in aanvul-woorden.tsv. The rest were rejected on purpose: proper nouns, transparent inflections, diminutives, extraction artefacts and words below zipf 2.0. Words already present were not touched — their existing gloss and level won.

Read the other way round, the list already covered most of what the three courses teach: 88% of Nederlands in gang's 1,013 headwords, 86% of the TaalCompleet B1 themes 1–5 list, and 80% of the Nederlands in actie register.

Where the added coursebook words sit on the frequency axis is the telling part. Of the 321 les:* rows now in woorden.tsv, only 85 of the 316 with a wordfreq score reach zipf 3.35 — the cut-off the frequency rule used — and 231 sit below it. Their median zipf is 2.99, and 210 of the 321 land in Verdieping. By source the medians are close: 3.16 (Nederlands in gang), 3.11 (TaalCompleet themes 1–5) and 2.77 (the Nederlands in actie register). In other words, the coursebooks pull in a low-frequency tail that a pure frequency rule discards: these are words a learner is taught at A1–B1 even though the corpus counts them as rare. This is the case for keeping a coursebook source beside the frequency source, and it is why 321 rows of 10,000 are there.

Reproducing the selection without changing anything

python3 -m pip install --target /tmp/wf wordfreq
python3 tools/lexicon/verrijk-woorden.py --wordfreq /tmp/wf --report

--report scores the pool and prints the arithmetic above — pool size, how many are already present, the cut-off, how many sit on it, what the next band would cost — and exits without writing. A plain run adds words and stops once the target is reached.

Known defects in the result

  • 41 wrong English glosses on bijlage8 rows, with row numbers, in Docs/lexicon/gloss-audit.html. Cause is the PDF extraction; fix by hand, do not regenerate.
  • rechercheur's gloss was corrected by hand in woorden.tsv, so a full regeneration reverts it. The luifel domain correction lives in domein.py and survives.
  • Theme accuracy is weak on kaikki rows: 16 of 33 sampled named themes were wrong. domein is the axis to browse by; thema on those rows is a hint.
  • The boundary is arbitrary. 54 of 58 words at zipf 3.35 got in by alphabetical order. Do not treat inclusion or exclusion at the tail as meaningful.
