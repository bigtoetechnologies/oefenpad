Where the next ~3,000 words can come from

Every open source inspected, what it actually contains, what it is licensed under, and the themed gap none of them can close.

Written 16 November 2026. Research only: nothing here changes woorden.tsv, woorden-woordsoort.tsv, woorden-thema.tsv, extraheer-woorden.py or thema.py. The extracted words are in tools/lexicon/kandidaten.tsv.

The answer in one paragraph

10,000 ordinary lemmas is reachable; 10,000 themed lemmas is not, from open sources. kaikki.org's English-Wiktionary extraction for Dutch gives English glosses, part of speech and lemmas under a licence that permits commercial use, and after removing names, inflected forms, obsolete registers and words already on the list it still yields 10,442 new lemmas with clean English glosses. That number is larger than the 2,893 the project needs, so the lemma count is no longer the constraint. The constraint is that only about 2,500 of those new lemmas land in one of the 24 product themes, and about a quarter of those landings are wrong when you read them. The themed gap (roughly 4,800 themed everyday words, of which about 1,400 exist) cannot be closed by any open source found here. It needs a hand-built list, or a theme tag written by a human against a topic list that open data does not carry.

How 10,442 was produced, and why it is not padded

Starting from all 146,790 kaikki entries:

  1. Keep noun, verb, adj, adv only.
  2. Drop every entry whose senses are form_of or alt_of — that is what makes the result a lemma list rather than a form list.
  3. Drop a word if every sense is tagged rare, archaic, obsolete, dated, historical, slang, neologism, nonstandard, misspelling, humorous, vulgar, derogatory, offensive, childish or bureaucratese.
  4. Drop any gloss that is itself a form description ("…indicative of X"), a spelling note ("Synonym of", "Official spelling of"), or a cross-reference.
  5. Drop anything already in woorden.tsv, matching on the lemma with the (verb)/(noun) disambiguator stripped.
  6. Apply wordfreq's Dutch zipf score as an everyday-ness gate at ≥ 2.5 (roughly "occurs at least occasionally in edited text"). This alone removed 11,270 unattested and 10,600 near-silent dictionary entries.
  7. Drop English loanwords that the corpus frequencies over-supply (house, girl, big, star, party) unless their Dutch frequency is very high.
  8. Drop words that kaikki also documents as an inflected form, at moderate frequency, to avoid waren, gemaakt, wilde-type homographs.

That leaves 10,442 rows, every one with a non-empty English gloss, no duplicates, and zero overlap with woorden.tsv (checked). Of those, 4,949 have a zipf ≥ 3.0 — that is, comfortably common words, the kind the product wants for A2–B1. A sampling of the ≥ 3.0 band: informatie, postkantoor, verblijfsvergunning, id-kaart, ov-chipkaart, registratie, annuleren, afzeggen, zorgzaam, kraamzorg, makkelijk, vergeet-mij-niet, auto-ongeluk, zwart-wit, mee-eten, kant-en-klaar, sleutelwoord, huurwoning, buurthuis, boodschappenlijst, gesprek, opleiding.

The list is not padded, but it is not clean either. The tail between zipf 2.5 and 3.0 contains encyclopaedic and specialist words (zeeniveau, belastingdruk, conservatorium) that are legitimate lemmas but not A1–B1 teaching material. Anyone merging this must apply a further judgement step, by cut-off on rank or by hand. I have deliberately not trimmed the file to disguise that; the report states the numbers at each cut-off so the decision is made on evidence:

Gate New lemmas Running total with the existing 7,107
zipf ≥ 2.5 (the file as shipped) 10,442 17,549
zipf ≥ 3.0 (comfortably common only) 4,949 12,056
zipf ≥ 3.5 1,624 8,731

So 10,000 is reachable, and reachable from words a learner would actually meet — the zipf ≥ 3.0 band alone gives 12,056, needing only ~2,000 more on top of 7,107 against 4,949 available. There is roughly a 2.7× margin at that gate. Even the strictest reasonable cut (zipf ≥ 3.5) plus the existing list reaches 8,731, and closing the last 1,300 from the 2.5–3.5 band is easy.

What the 24 themes still need

The lemma target is met. The themed target in Docs/product.html §12 is not, and this is the honest bad news.

Total in a world theme: 1,411 now → 3,956 after, against a §12 target of ~4,800 across 24 themes at ~200 each.

Three qualifications, none of them in the candidates' favour:

  1. The classifier is a keyword matcher, and dictionary glosses trip it. In Boodschappen it files klimaatverandering (on "change"), amendement (on "change"), omkleden (on "change"), magazijn (on "store") and slager, only one of which belongs. In Formulieren it files tankstation, pompstation and vulling because their glosses contain "fill". In Buurt it files beitel (a chisel, on "block") and galerij (an art gallery). I estimate precision as roughly 70–80% in the mid-size themes and below 50% in Formulieren, Buurt and De weg. The "after" column above is therefore an overcount of usable themed words, probably by 500–700.
  2. The thin themes are thin for a reason that no open word list fixes. Buurt, Afspraken, Bellen, Zorg voor iemand anders, Solliciteren and Formulieren are situations, not semantic domains. Their vocabulary is mostly the same high-frequency general words (deur, brief, afspreken, belasting) already counted under algemeen, and adding 10,000 lemmas does not change that. Getting Buurt to 200 means deciding which words belong to the neighbourhood and listing them — that is editorial work, not data sourcing.
  3. kaikki's topics field does not rescue this. Only 5,360 of 146,790 entries carry topics at all, and the everyday words that matter do not: supermarkt, dokter, buurman, apotheek, postkantoor, huurwoning have none. A topic-driven themer cannot be built from this.

What would actually close the themed gap, in order of cost:

  • The kandidaten.tsv rows that the classifier places in a theme, sorted by frequency, hand-checked theme by theme. That is ~3,900 rows to review, a realistic two or three days, and yields maybe 2,700 usable ones. It gets most themes past 150 and a handful past 200.
  • The ISK 600-word alphabetisation list (https://www.wikiwijs.nl/app/uploads/ISK_startwoordenschat-600-woorden.pdf) is grouped by exactly the right situations (Ik ga naar school, Ik heb vrije tijd, …). Its licence is unstated, so it can be used as a checklist to write against, not copied. This is the single most useful artefact found for the themed problem and it cannot be shipped.
  • Dutch municipal and government "klare taal" corpora (gemeente, DUO, DigiD explainers) are the right register for Gemeente en post, Formulieren, Afspraken — but the ones checked are government copyright, not CC.
  • Otherwise: write the themed lists. At ~200 words × 6 thin themes, that is ~1,200 rows of hand work with English glosses — less than one day for a competent editor, and the only route that produces a list matching the product's own themes.

Attribution obligations before this ships

The CC BY-SA 4.0 obligation is real and applies to whatever is built from kaikki:

  • Credit "English Wiktionary, CC BY-SA 4.0, via kaikki.org/wiktextract" in the site's credits or colophon, with a link to the licence.
  • The derived gloss list becomes Adapted Material and must itself be shared under CC BY-SA 4.0. If kandidaten.tsv is merged into woorden.tsv and that file is published, it must carry the BY-SA notice. Keeping woorden.tsv internal and shipping only the game rounds does not escape this, because the glosses reach the learner.
  • If wordfreq rankings are baked into a shipped ranking, add Robyn Speer, SUBTLEX (Brysbaert et al.) and OpenSubtitles attribution per NOTICE.md.
  • OpenTaal, if used as a spelling oracle, needs its BSD-3 or CC BY 3.0 notice.
  • The coursebook additions in aanvul-woorden.tsv (les:nig, les:taalcompleet, les:register) do not create an attribution debt: a list of words in a language is not copyrightable, and the lemmas were normalised, selected against our own list and re-levelled rather than copied in the books' order or selection. Credit the coursebooks as sources in the documentation, not as licensors.

The owner settled this on 18 November 2026: the lexical content is free to reuse, and the only live licence obligation is the CC BY-SA copyleft that merging kaikki into woorden.tsv creates.
