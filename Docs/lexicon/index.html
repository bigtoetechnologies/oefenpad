Lexicon

The 10,000-lemma Dutch list behind the themes: its files, the pipeline that builds them, and what each axis does and does not mean.

Docs/lexicon/index.html

A 10,000-lemma Dutch vocabulary list, classified by part of speech and by the product's themes. It is evidence and data, not a learner surface: a page that says "learn these 4,000 words" makes the learner read before practising, which the project rules call suspect. Nothing in the site reads these files yet.

tools/lexicon/woorden.tsv is the master word list for this project. It and the files derived from it are the only authoritative answer to "which words does the product know, and at what level". woorden-niveau.tsv is the word-to-level file. A word list that lives anywhere else — including the word banks in the BigToe Games repository — is not this vocabulary.

The files

woorden.tsv is the master. The five column files under it are generated from it by the scripts in this directory and are row-aligned to it: same nl values, same order, 10,000 data rows each. woorden-niveau.tsv is the one that assigns the level. The candidate and KNM TSVs in the same table are input pools that feed the master list, and index.html is the generated viewer.

File Rows Shape
woorden.tsv 10,000 nl en niveau top2000 herkomst — the master list
woorden-niveau.tsv 10,000 nl niveau rang grondslag — our five frequency bands (see below)
woorden-frequentie.tsv 10,000 nl zipf rang — the wordfreq score the bands are cut from
woorden-woordsoort.tsv 10,000 nl pos en — same rows, same order
woorden-thema.tsv 10,000 nl thema thema_ook prioriteit en — same rows, same order
woorden-domein.tsv 10,000 nl domein en — semantic domain, same rows, same order
kandidaten.tsv 10,442 nl en bron licentie — the vetted online pool the top-up draws from
knm-kandidaten.tsv 321 nl en knm_thema knm_sectie — the TaalCompleet KNM word lists
knm-thema.tsv 279 nl thema prioriteit waarom — hand-checked theme for the KNM words
index.html — generated viewer for all of the above (see below)
Docs/lexicon/bronnen.html — where every row came from and the exact selection rule — read this before trusting a count
Docs/lexicon/aanvulling.html — the source and licence research behind kandidaten.tsv

The six 10,000-row files are row-aligned by construction: the nl column is byte-identical and in the same order. Each script asserts this.

Reading them without a terminal

Open tools/lexicon/index.html in a browser. It is one self-contained file — 2.5 MB, no network, no server, works by double-click — with the whole 10,000-row list joined by position, plus tabs for the KNM list and the candidate pool. It offers search (case- and accent-insensitive), filters on pos, thema, domein, prioriteit, niveau and herkomst, sortable columns, and a summary panel with the per-theme and per-domain counts.

It is generated, never hand-edited:

python3 tools/lexicon/bouw-viewer.py     # -> tools/lexicon/index.html

Re-run it after any change to the TSVs; the build asserts the headers, the row counts and the row alignment, and fails if a hand-edit has broken the data.

Every facet must offer a (geen) option for a value that can be blank. The first version of the niveau filter did not, and because that column was the coursebook tier — blank for 4,838 rows — those rows were counted in all (N) and appeared under no option. The counts looked wrong and were. trede now shows (geen), every facet partitions exactly, and a throwaway headless harness confirmed it against the rendered page.

pos is one of noun, verb, adj, adv, name, function, interjection, phrase. thema is one of the 24 themes in Docs/product.html §12 plus algemeen, naam and plaats. domein is one of the 30 semantic domains listed in domein.py. prioriteit is kern, nuttig or laag. niveau in the viewer is ours (five frequency bands); the coursebook's own A1/B1/open tier is shown beside it as trede. The niveau column in woorden.tsv is that tier, not a level — see §"Our level scale".

The pipeline

Run in this order. Every step reads and rewrites files in this directory.

# 1. the coursebook appendix + the games' word banks -> woorden.tsv (7,107)
#    the appendix PDF is not in the repo; pass its path
python3 tools/lexicon/extraheer-woorden.py \
    "/path/to/Bijlage-8-Frequente woorden (1).pdf"

# 2. + the KNM vocabulary -> woorden.tsv (7,232)
python3 tools/lexicon/knm-woorden.py

# 3. + coursebook vocabulary the frequency cut misses -> woorden.tsv (7,556)
python3 tools/lexicon/aanvul-woorden.py

# 4. + the frequency extension -> woorden.tsv (10,000)
python3 -m pip install --target /tmp/wf wordfreq
python3 tools/lexicon/verrijk-woorden.py --wordfreq /tmp/wf --target 10000

# 5. part of speech -> woorden-woordsoort.tsv
python3 tools/lexicon/woordsoort.py

# 6. theme and priority -> woorden-thema.tsv
python3 tools/lexicon/thema.py

# 7. semantic domain -> woorden-domein.tsv (needs 1 and 6)
python3 tools/lexicon/domein.py

# 8. frequency score -> woorden-frequentie.tsv (needs 1)
#    wordfreq is installed once, to a throwaway directory
python3 -m pip install --target /tmp/wf wordfreq
python3 tools/lexicon/frequentie.py --wordfreq /tmp/wf

# 9. our five bands -> woorden-niveau.tsv (needs 1 and 8)
python3 tools/lexicon/niveau.py

Re-running step 1 rewrites woorden.tsv from scratch and drops everything steps 2, 3 and 4 added. Run 2, 3 and 4 again afterwards. Steps 5–9 must then be re-run because they are keyed on row order.

Each step is deterministic. No network access after the pip install.

Where the words come from

herkomst Rows Source
bijlage8 4,709 Nederlands in actie, Bijlage 8 — the 5,000 most frequent Dutch lemmas, C. Tiberius & T. Schoonheim (2014), A Frequency Dictionary of Dutch, Routledge. Grading read from the page fill: light blue = Nederlands in gang (A1), orange = Nederlands in actie (B1), unmarked = open
spel:<game> 2,398 the six games' own word banks, in server/Oefenpad.Resources/Games/Data
kaikki 2,444 English Wiktionary via kaikki.org, chosen by wordfreq zipf rank, cut-off 3.35
knm 125 the TaalCompleet KNM word lists (Nederlands + Engels), extracted to knm-kandidaten.tsv
les:* 321 curated coursebook vocabulary in aanvul-woorden.tsv: Nederlands in gang (les:nig), the Nederlands in actie register (les:register) and TaalCompleet B1 themes 1–5 (les:taalcompleet)
handmatig 3 confirmed high-frequency gaps added by hand

These categories partition the 10,000 exactly. bijlage8 counts rows whose only source is the appendix; the 453 appendix words that are also in a game bank are counted under spel, and spel itself is any row in at least one bank (2,398).

niveau = open in woorden.tsv means "in the 5,000 most frequent lemmas but taught in neither coursebook", a candidate pool for B1+. It is not a B2 label, and the viewer calls that column trede for exactly that reason. No public B2 word list exists: CEFR publishes none, the Groene Boekje is spelling only, and SUBTLEX-NL, Routledge and NT2Lex are all restricted (NT2Lex is CC BY-NC-SA, out on licence grounds). B2 receptive vocabulary is usually put at 8,000–10,000 lemmas, which is where the 10,000 target comes from — it is a target, not a measurement.

The kaikki 2,444 were not chosen one by one. They are the most frequent 2,444 of the 10,249 usable candidates, ranked with wordfreq and cut at zipf 3.35; 7,805 candidates were left unused. Docs/lexicon/bronnen.html has the arithmetic, the source table and the provenance counts, and python3 tools/lexicon/verrijk-woorden.py --wordfreq /tmp/wf311 --report reproduces it without writing anything.

Our level scale

The coursebook's tiering (A1, B1, open) is not a level. It says which of two books teaches a word, it borrows CEFR's names without CEFR's meaning, and it is blank for 4,838 of the 10,000 rows. niveau.py therefore writes a second, separate axis — niveau, ours, cut by frequency rank from woorden-frequentie.tsv:

band rank roughly rows
Start 1–1,000 A0–A1 1,006
Basis 1,001–2,500 A1–A2 1,506
Midden 2,501–5,000 A2–B1 2,505
Gevorderd 5,001–8,000 B1–B2 3,015
Verdieping 8,001–10,000 B2 and past it 1,968

Every row has exactly one band, which is why the viewer's filter counts add up. The rank is wordfreq's Dutch score, not the appendix order, so the band answers "when does a learner meet this word", not "where does a coursebook put it". The trede column keeps the coursebook answer beside it.

These cut points were settled on 17 November 2026. They are a product decision, not a data one, and the alternatives were weighed: bands taken from the appendix's own 5,000 tiers with the game banks weighted differently, and even 2,000-word bands instead of 1,000 / 1,500 / 2,500 / 3,000 / 2,000. Both were set aside — an uneven ladder follows the rate at which vocabulary actually accumulates (fast at the start, slow at the top), and the appendix has no tiering above 5,000 to borrow. The cost of the choice is the second consequence below: the boundaries are ranks in this list, so growth to 12,000 moves them.

The CEFR ranges are an approximation of convenience, not a measurement. They are checked against the appendix tiers, and the check is unflattering:

  • 78% of the words the A1 coursebook teaches are in Start or Basis (728 of 928). The rest are frequent enough to be in Midden or beyond.
  • 43% of the B1 coursebook's words are in Midden or beyond (329 of 766). A B1 coursebook teaches a lot of very frequent words, because B1 is about using them, not only meeting them.

So a band is a frequency statement. Never print a CEFR label next to a lemma on the strength of its band; the ranges above exist so the scale can be read at a glance, not so a word can be certified.

190 lemmas have no wordfreq score at all — game compounds such as ankylosauride, and a few misspellings such as belangstellilng. They rank last and land in Verdieping, which is what "absent from the corpus" means. The 22 of them that are appendix words, the 5 that are KNM words and the 5 that a coursebook teaches take their band from that evidence instead; grondslag in woorden-niveau.tsv says which rule fired (rang, trede, knm, les, spel). niveau.py --why <word> prints it.

Two consequences worth knowing before this axis is used for planning:

  • The game banks are not a frequency ladder. With domein = Animals and thema = algemeen, 294 of 439 words are Verdieping: the games deliberately carry exotic species (ankylosaurus, zeekomkommer). Pictures make a rare word learnable; frequency still calls it rare.
  • The cut points are ranks in this 10,000. Growing the list to 12,000 moves every boundary. Re-run frequentie.py and niveau.py and re-read the calibration table before trusting the bands after a rebuild.

Licence — settled

A list of words in a language is not copyrightable. Neither the choice of Dutch lemmas nor their order can be owned, and this is not any single source's list: it is rearranged, extended from Wiktionary and the games, and re-levelled on our own frequency scale. The coursebook extracts are used to cross-check our coverage and to supply candidate lemmas — not reproduced as their lists.

  • bijlage8, knm and les:* rows come from coursebooks. The individual words are facts about Dutch, not protected expression; the books' prose, illustrations and their selection as a whole are, and we copy neither.
  • kaikki rows are CC BY-SA 4.0 + GFDL. Because they are merged into the same files, the three 10,000-row files are copyleft.
  • wordfreq supplies the ranking only. Its data derives from Wikipedia, ParaCrawl, OpenSubtitles, SUBTLEX-NL and Google Books Ngrams.

The attribution wording, and what each obligation attaches to, is in Docs/lexicon/bronnen.html §Attribution and Docs/lexicon/aanvulling.html §Attribution.

This is the owner's ruling of 18 November 2026, recorded so it is not relitigated. The lexical content is free to reuse; the only live obligation is the CC BY-SA copyleft that arrives with the kaikki rows.

How good the classification is

Measured on 16 November 2026 by hand-scoring fixed-seed samples; re-run the sample print at the end of thema.py to check again. Re-checked on 18 November 2026 against the rebuilt list: the same seed draws 77 appendix-backed rows and 23 kaikki rows, and the scores below still hold — one adjective read as a noun in the first group (kapot), three errors in the second (proces-verbaal, ondersteboven, rechtdoor). On 20 November 2026 a curated override block in woordsoort.py retagged 24 lemmas the word-type cards use, those three included; the scores above predate it.

  • part of speech: 98% correct on appendix rows, 88% on kaikki rows. The errors are adjectives tagged noun (flexibel, getalenteerd, doelgericht), adverbs tagged noun (jazeker, idem), and -weg compounds tagged adv.
  • theme: reliable on the appendix and games rows, because the classifier's seed tables were built for them. On kaikki rows it is weak: in a sample of 33 named themes, 16 were wrong (terugweg → Het lichaam). Treat a kaikki row's theme as a hint, not a fact.
  • knm rows carry the hand-checked theme in knm-thema.tsv; thema.py reads it and nothing else assigns those rows.
  • priority: kaikki rows are capped at nuttig because they lie beyond the appendix's 5,000 most frequent lemmas, so they cannot be the teaching core. Before that rule, 95 of 100 sampled kaikki rows were kern and laag held 2 rows out of 2,893.

The domain axis

thema is a situation: a supermarket, a doctor's appointment, a form. It leaves 7,360 words in algemeen, which is a bucket, not a category — too large to browse or to build a theme around. domein is the semantic field: what kind of thing the word names, whatever the situation.

It is a second axis, not a replacement. The product's theme list is fixed by Docs/product.html §12 and is not extended here.

domein.py is deterministic: structural rules first, then ~100 curated overrides, then 27 keyword rules, then 13 Dutch compound suffix rules, then the game seed, then a coarse theme seed, then Abstract & general. --why <word> prints which step fired.

Current distribution: only 3,236 of 10,000 (32.4%) are Abstract & general. The other 6,764 spread across the remaining 29 labels; the largest is Animals at 446 (4.5%), the smallest War & military at 79 (0.8%). All 30 labels are used.

Accuracy, from three blind stratified audits by an independent agent (8, 12 and 12 random rows per label, fresh seeds): 69–81% correct, 12–20% acceptable, 6.4–12.5% wrong. The third audit, run after the fixes below, put it at 81.4% correct, 12.2% acceptable, 6.4% wrong on 360 rows.

The collision fixes came in two rounds on 17 November 2026: remove the ambiguous alternative from the rule (bare court from Sport, bare policy from Money, minister from Religion, stage and example from Arts and Character, department from Education) or add a curated override where the gloss itself misleads (bewerkstelligen, opstarten, voorbeeld). Notable structural changes: body-part hand is no longer matched by the general Body rule, which was pulling in every gloss containing "on the other hand".

The nine concrete domains the audits found clean are Animals, Arts & media, Food & drink, House & home, Movement & position, Names, Technology & tools, Time and Transport & travel.

An earlier method — slicing the list into six files and having an agent label them — was discarded on 17 November 2026: it was not reproducible and its labels were close to random (atoom → Names, zus → Food & drink). The slices were deleted. Do not restore that method. domein.py is the only source of woorden-domein.tsv.

The theme gap, stated plainly

Only 2,258 of 10,000 words sit in one of the 24 product themes. The other 7,742 are algemeen (7,360), naam (313) or plaats (69). This is the gap the domain axis above addresses for browsing and for content planning; it does not change the theme labels themselves.

This is not a classifier failure to be tuned away. The 24 themes are situations and concrete domains — a supermarket, a doctor's appointment, a form — and the lemmas that a 10,000-word B2 list adds beyond the coursebook are mostly abstract (toepassing, polarisatie, voorwaarde). They have no home in the 24, and algemeen is the honest label for them.

Seven themes remain thin and cannot be filled from open word lists: Bellen (8), Afspraken (8), Buurt (11), Klachten (15), Solliciteren (16), Formulieren (19), Zorg voor iemand anders (20). They name situations rather than semantic domains, so they have to be drafted by hand from the material a learner actually meets. The KNM list was the one source that moved several of them (Formulieren 12 → 19, Solliciteren 9 → 16, Zorg voor iemand anders 10 → 20) and it is now exhausted.

Do not

  • Do not edit woorden.tsv by hand to fix a theme or a part of speech. Fix the table in thema.py / woordsoort.py and re-run, or the next run undoes it.
  • Do not re-run extraheer-woorden.py without running steps 2 and 3 after it.
  • Do not treat trede as a CEFR label. open means "not taught in either coursebook"; A1 and B1 mean "filled light blue / orange in Bijlage 8".
  • Do not treat niveau as a measurement either: it is a frequency band with an approximate CEFR range attached, not a level test result.
  • Do not serve this directory from the web app. Only assets/ and the WebUI wwwroot are exposed, deliberately.
