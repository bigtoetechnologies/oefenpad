Status: pilot, agreed 2026-11-06, built 2026-11-08, now a React game at client/src/games/prepositions/ with its content in server/Oefenpad.Resources/Games/Data/prepositions.json. Supersedes the "Preposition Playground" sketch in games.html. Canonical name: Voorzetselpaden / Preposition Paths, per designguide.html and game-catalogue.html.

This file is the spec and the reasoning. prepositions-implementation.html describes the code as it shipped, including the fields that changed. Where the two differ, the implementation doc is right.

The A0–A1 slice — twenty-eight chunks over ninety-one example scenes — is the first slice of a larger curriculum. prepositions-roadmap.html plans the path to 700 frames and B2, and states which of those numbers are productive and which are receptive.

The problem

A matching drill asks "which preposition follows this verb?". That tests a pairing. It does not test meaning. uitkijken naar is not the sum of uitkijken + naar; it means to look forward to. uitkijken voor means something else. A learner who taps naar because it was on the card has not learned either chunk.

The activity has two jobs:

  1. teach what the chunk means in a situation, and
  2. teach the form, including the separable prefix and the pronominal form.

Design principle

Make the situation the answer, and the preposition the consequence.

  • Teach chunks in contrast sets: uitkijken naar beside uitkijken voor, denken aan beside denken over.
  • Use scenes, not translations, as the main carrier of meaning. English glosses are a fallback, per decisions.html.
  • Ask a concept question that can only be answered if the learner understood the chunk: positief of negatief?, nu of later?, wie doet het?
  • Only then ask for the form, with the prefix split.

One path, four stops

The four modes share one dataset. A session is a short path, not four separate games.

Stop Learner does Feedback Mode
Ontdek Sees a scene, the sentence and the gloss once None; this is input R
Kies het beeld Picks the scene that matches a sentence Shows the sentence on the scene R
Klopt de zin? Says whether a sentence fits a scene; the false item is the contrast partner Names the chunk that would fit, then asks a concept question R
Bouw de zin Builds the sentence from tiles, with the separable prefix at the end Points at the misplaced prefix, not just "wrong" P

Optional warm-up: Hetzelfde of anders? — two sentences, same verb, different preposition; same meaning or different? This is the cheapest way to show that the preposition carries meaning. Not built.

Optional fifth stop: Krimp — transform a full sentence into its pronominal form: Ik denk aan jou → Ik denk eraan; Ik kijk uit naar de vakantie → Ik kijk ernaar uit. It uses the same scene and the pronominal field. This is where the er + preposition chunk is learned. Not built; the shipped game prints the pronominal form as a Korter: line on the Ontdek card instead.

Example: uitkijken naar / uitkijken voor

Ontdek. Scene: a person with a suitcase, smiling at a calendar with a circled day. Sentence: Ik kijk uit naar de vakantie. Gloss: to look forward to. Then the second scene: a child at the curb, a car approaching. Sentence: Kijk uit voor de auto! Gloss: to watch out for.

Klopt de zin?

Scene Sentence Answer
Holiday calendar Ik kijk uit naar de vakantie. Klopt
Holiday calendar Ik kijk uit voor de vakantie. Klopt niet
Child at the curb Kijk uit voor de auto! Klopt
Child at the curb Kijk uit naar de auto! Klopt niet

Then the concept questions: Is het positief of negatief? Gaat het over nu of later? A learner can answer those without knowing the word "preposition".

Bouw de zin. Scene: holiday calendar. Tiles: Ik · kijk · uit · naar · de · vakantie. The frame is shown as two ghost slots, uitkijken ____ ... ____. If uit lands next to kijk, the feedback is "uit hoort achteraan".

Session flow

A pilot session was scoped at about six minutes, across a contrast pair. As shipped, a two-frame level is 8 scored answers (Ontdek is input and unscored), vragen with three frames is 12, and a single-frame level — ophouden, zorgen, meedoen, zoeken, last hebben, houden van, zin in, bang voor — is 3; the table is the design intent, not a measurement.

Step Time Items
Ontdek 30 sec 2 contrast scenes (3 in denken, which adds eraan denken)
Kies het beeld 1 min 2 sentences, 3 scene options each
Klopt de zin? 2 min 4 true/false items, all minimal pairs (6 in vragen); skipped when the level has no pair
Bouw de zin 2 min 2 sentences, one per accepted bouw line
Flitskaarten 1 min Not built; nothing is persisted

The session ends on retrieval of the items the learner got wrong, not on new material. That is the bridge to the mistakes queue in game-catalogue.html, and it is still only a design.

Progression

Do not start with uitkijken naar. Layer the difficulty:

  1. Non-separable, one frame — wachten op, denken aan.
  2. Non-separable, two frames — denken aan / denken over, praten met / praten over.
  3. Separable, one frame — ophouden met (Ik hou op met roken.).
  4. Separable, two frames — uitkijken naar / uitkijken voor.
  5. Pronominal — eraan denken, ernaar uitkijken, Waar denk je aan?

Batch 2 adds three more pairs — werken bij / werken aan, klaar met / klaar voor, vragen aan / vragen om — and four singles: zorgen voor, meedoen met, zoeken naar, last hebben van. They use the same four stops; the singles skip Klopt, so they have no concept question.

Batch 3 adds three more pairs — blij met / blij voor, boos op / boos over, klagen over / klagen bij — and three singles: houden van, zin in, bang voor. It also adds vragen naar to the existing vragen level, making it the pilot's only three-frame set: three Kies items, six Klopt items, three Bouw items. vragen naar names vragen aan as its one contrast partner, because vragen aan and vragen om are already a pair.

wachten op / wachten voor is a useful side contrast: the first is the idiomatic "wait for", the second is the spatial "wait in front of". It shows that not every voor is a fixed chunk.

Data model

The unit is a frame, not a verb: wachten op, not wachten. A scene is a separate record so the alt text has a home and one image can serve more than one frame. scenes[].file names a picture under assets/voorzetselpaden/.

The shipped shape — every field, and each place it differs from what this spec proposed — is in prepositions-implementation.html. Read it there rather than here; a second copy of the schema is how the two got out of step once already.

The decisions that still govern the data:

  • The frame carries its own separable and prefix. There is no verb table in the pilot and no join at render time. If a shared verb table appears later, a checker has to keep the two consistent.
  • contrast points at another frame. That single field is what lets the game generate a minimal-pair false item without a hand-written distractor list, and a null contrast is what removes the Klopt stop from a level.
  • A frame carries its accepted sentences as tokens. One array per accepted sentence, so a separable frame can be practised on two persons without the engine knowing any grammar.

Feedback rules

  • In the practice modes, never reveal the gloss before the learner has answered the meaning question. Ontdek is the exception: it is the input card. The scene is the clue; the gloss is the confirmation.
  • A wrong preposition is named, not just marked: "Dit is uitkijken voor, niet uitkijken naar."
  • A misplaced separable prefix is a position error, not a vocabulary error: "uit hoort achteraan."
  • A correct answer shows the scene, the full sentence, the chunk and the gloss together, so the form and the meaning are reconnected.
  • Klopt de zin? must use the contrast partner as the false item. A random wrong preposition teaches nothing about this chunk.

What the game checks

Shipped:

  • Kies het beeld and Klopt de zin? are table lookups; no parsing.
  • Bouw de zin compares token order and checks the position of the prefix separately. It does not share code with Zinsbouwer — that game does not exist yet, so only the tile-and-table approach carried over.
  • A wrong answer names the frame it belongs to, never just "wrong".

Designed and not built:

  • Krimp compares the learner's sentence with pronominal.nl; it is a fixed-answer transformation in v1.
  • Hetzelfde of anders? compares two frame ids and their glosses.
  • Flitskaarten stores misses by frame id, so a contrast pair stays together in the review queue.

Scope and non-goals

  • A0 and A1 ship twenty-eight chunks, each with two to four example scenes. That is the 30–50 chunk target of the survival-kit pillar as far as the first two bands reach; A2 (Voorzetselpaden/a2.html) is written but not yet drawn.
  • No audio in v1. Listening is blocked by decisions.html open question 6.
  • Not a full preposition table, and not a translation drill. The gloss is a fallback, never the main task.
  • aandenken is a noun ("souvenir"), not a verb + preposition chunk. If it is wanted, it needs its own vocabulary card; it is not part of this set.
  • opbellen, aankomen and other separable verbs without a fixed preposition belong to the verb/word-order work. They are useful preparation, but they are not frames in this activity.

Open questions

  • The stop bar hides the Klopt de zin? entry on a chunk without a contrast partner rather than dimming it. Three entries in a four-column habit is honest, but a learner who did another level first may read the gap as a bug.
  • How many example scenes of one chunk should a session hold before it becomes repetition? The pilot ships two to four and does not answer this.
  • The concept question was placed after the true/false answer. The shipped game asks it there, and only at the Klopt stop: a chunk with no contrast partner has no Klopt stop, so its concept question never appears. Worth revisiting when Krimp is built.
  • Does the review queue need to keep contrast partners together, or is it enough to store the frame id and let the scheduler decide?

Pilot images

The scene briefs are in voorzetselpaden/a0-a2.html, one card per frame. The images are delivered and in place; two batch-1 images are flagged there for a redraw.
