How the cards work

Fixed expressions are taught in contrast sets: two to four chunks that one visible element separates (op tijd / te laat, aan zee / op zee). Every drawing in a set is the same picture with one thing changed, so the learner sees the pair, not the phrase.

A card carries the chunk, an English gloss, the scene brief, the one mark that must read, the near misses, the contrast note naming its partner, an example sentence, the alt text, a register flag (neutral, spoken, formal) and a literal or figurative flag. Each set also carries a fixed frame and one Dutch sentence using every member.

What counts as a chunk here

A chunk has a fixed form: no article (aan tafel, op zee), a frozen word (ter plekke, te voet) or a preposition the learner cannot predict (op het platteland). Free preposition-plus-noun combinations, where any noun fits (in de trein, zonder melk), belong to the single-preposition cards in Voorzetselpaden, and single adverbs belong to Bijwoorden. Voorzetselpaden holds verb-plus-preposition chunks; no card here repeats one of its chunks, example sentences or scenes, and the checker tests that. Three cards have their partner drawn in Bijwoorden and point to it instead of drawing it twice.

Chunks that need a sequence or a sign (om de beurt, voor het eerst, te koop) are written up as SKIP and listed below.

How a set was placed

The lexicon holds single words, so a chunk is ranked by its most frequent content word (tools/lexicon/woorden-frequentie.tsv: A0 up to 250, A1 251–1000, A2 1001–2500, B1 2501–5000, B2 5001–10000). Lexicon rows split by part of speech are keyed explicitly (pas(noun), arm(noun)); a set whose content words are all missing from the 10,000-lemma list has no rank and is placed by an override. Prepositions and articles are left out on purpose: they would put every chunk in A0. A set sits in the band of its most frequent member. Banding is editorial, with frequency as a first pass: a rare second word (plafond, sok, arm) pulls a set up only where the overrides below say so, and sets keyed on a homograph or a pronoun (laat, kop, elkaar) are placed by hand. The overrides live in OVERRIDE in tools/_vu_tmp/select.py, each with its reason; the checker rejects an override that only repeats the band the ranks give:

Weakest pictures

Sets that lean on a small cue and need an artist's care: ochtends/avonds (half sun against a dark window and a lit lamp), the four stair cards (no arrows; the place on the stair and the way the body faces carry the meaning), in-de-pas/uit-de-pas (which knee one child of four raises, seen from the front) and in-draf/in-galop (the leg pattern of a moving horse).

B1 and B2 against the target

The target for the expansion of 20 November 2026 was at least 20 new contrast sets in B1 and 20 in B2. This family reached 7 new sets in B1 and 4 in B2, so it is short by 13 and 16.

Native check before art

  • door-de-bocht-gaan: the figurative sense 'give in' is the reviewer's recollection, not verified; 'kort door de bocht' is certain
  • in-de-knop: garden Dutch has both 'in de knop' and 'in knop'; if 'in knop' is the standard form, the chunk changes and loses its article like 'in bloei'
  • aan-de-kade: a collocation rather than a frozen chunk; kept as the partner of voor anker

Cards written up as SKIP

  • te-koop (A1, koop): the meaning lives in a sign or a price board, and no text or numbers may appear in the art; a house alone shows nothing.
  • te-huur (A1, koop): the meaning lives in a sign, and no text may appear in the art; the contrast with te koop cannot be drawn.
  • voor-het-eerst (A1, eerste): first time needs a before and an after; a single picture shows no earlier occasion and no memory may be drawn as text.
  • voor-het-laatst (A1, eerste): last time needs a sequence that has ended; a single picture cannot show that nothing follows.
  • in-plaats-van (A2, plaats): replacement needs a before and an after; one picture of an apple and a pushed-away banana reads as a choice or as two fruits, not as one thing taking the place of another.
  • om-de-beurt (A2, beurt): taking turns is alternation over time; one picture cannot show who went first or that the roles will swap.
  • tegelijk (A2, beurt): at the same time is only meaningful against another moment; a single picture of two people acting looks the same as any pair.
  • met-de-klok-mee (B1, klok-draai): the direction of turning needs motion, or arrows and a clock reference; a still runner seen from above shows no direction. Waits for animation.
  • tegen-de-klok-in (B1, klok-draai): the direction of turning needs motion, or arrows and a clock reference; a still runner seen from above shows no direction. Waits for animation.
