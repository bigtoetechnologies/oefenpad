Status: first draft, 16 November 2026. Built by interview with the owner; sections marked OPEN are unanswered questions, not blanks to guess at. This document is the single source for what the product is and who it is for. Everything else in Docs/ is implementation detail or history.

1. What this is, in one sentence

Oefenpad turns the Dutch you have already met into words you can reach for mid-sentence — through a repetitive process that pushes them into memory.

2. The gap we fill

Courses teach. They do not get people to fluent.

The learner who arrives here has usually done some Dutch already — a course, an inburgering intake, a year of Duolingo, a job where people speak English at them. They can follow the class and then run out of words halfway through a sentence.

Owner's framing, 16 November 2026:

Most courses teach just enough vocabulary to use the grammar they are teaching. Courses are roughly 90% grammar, but grammar is about 20% of the problem. The other 80% is the actual words people need.

So the gap is volume of the right words, and reps on them. The learner does not need a seventh explanation of om te + infinitive. They need two thousand more words they can produce without stopping, met often enough to stay.

Three consequences, and they bind the whole product:

  1. Vocabulary is the product; grammar is the minority. Grammar drilling stays, but it is the smaller slice, and it exists to serve the words rather than the words existing to demonstrate the rule.
  2. The unit of progress is "a word I can now use", not "a rule I now understand". That is what has to be visible on the screen.
  3. Repetition is the feature, not the padding. Meeting the same words again is the mechanism. It is not evidence that we ran out of content.

The owner's own evidence, 16 November — the sharpest statement of the gap in this document:

I passed inburgering and scored straight 10s in the A2 language tests, so I can get citizenship — and I still can't hold a conversation. That is the gap we're filling.

No market claim beats that. The exam can be passed perfectly and the conversation still fails, because the exam measured what the exam measures. It is also the reason §4's decision is not a retreat: we are not refusing to do exam prep, we are pointing at what exam prep leaves behind.

The destination is better fluency. Holding a conversation without drying up is what the learner wants and what the product aims at. See §3 for why that is the aim and not the promise.

Where the evidence comes from, honestly. Owner, 16 November: mostly the word is missing, sometimes it arrives too late, and rarely is it fear of the grammar — "but that's me, not others; I suspect from others that it's similar."

So the founding evidence is a sample of one plus anecdote, and §2 should be read that way. That is not a weakness to hide: the owner is also the first test subject (§11), and a hypothesis with a named test beats a survey nobody ran. But no content decision may be justified by "the owner finds it so" on its own.

Two things he reported that generalise well:

  • Prepositions are the worst case. They carry the most meaning per syllable and the least picture, which is why they are the flagship game.
  • Looking things up destroys the exercise. Television with a dictionary ends in zoning out. So an unknown word may never send the learner out of the task — see §6, rule 5.

A2 is a low ceiling, and that is the market. The owner's straight 10s came after a year of courses reaching almost B1, so the score measures a modest level. In his words, 16 November: "A2 is very low. The biggest gap after A2 is mostly an enormous amount of vocabulary that needs to be learned, and that's where we come in."

That defines the audience more sharply than §4 does: people who finished the course. Indicative sizes, approximate and not load-bearing: A2 ≈ 2,000 words, comfortable B1 ≈ 3,500–4,000. The gap is a few thousand words, and no course timetable has room for them.

It also validates the content target: 24 themes at ~200 words is ~4,800 words — the right size for exactly that gap, and the reason "2,000" was never the number.

Caveat, to be honest with ourselves: the 80/20 split is a product thesis, not a measured finding. It should be falsifiable by our own numbers — which is why vocabulary growth is the natural thing to report in §9.

3. The promise, and what we may claim

We do not teach you Dutch. We give you the words — and the reps that keep them.

Explicitly not the promise:

  • Not "learn Dutch in 24 hours" / "fluent in a month" — no speed claims.
  • Not "we'll teach you Dutch" — we are not a course, not a curriculum, not a teacher. No lessons, no units, no enrolment.

What we may claim, and why

The owner raised the temptation directly on 16 November: "we can't be 100% honest about what we offer or no one will buy it." I think that is the one place he is wrong, and the doc records the disagreement rather than settling it: overclaiming is what the competitors do, and it is the thing his own opening line rejected. Keeping the promise checkable is also the cheapest differentiator available — the learner has been sold "fluent in three months" already and it did not work.

Proposed rule, so honesty does not mean timid:

  • Claim outcomes, never mechanisms. "You will run out of words less often." "You will stop freezing at the counter." Not "we make you fluent", not "in eight weeks".
  • A claim we can half-prove with our own numbers beats a claim we cannot. "Members learn 400 new words in their first month" is a sales line and a measurement in §9.
  • Say what we are not. "No lessons. No certificate. No teacher." This is a selling position, not a disclaimer — it is the reason someone chooses us over a course for the fourth time.
  • Why we may not hint at the exam. §4 puts inburgering out of scope, so no copy, image or page may imply a pass, a level, or exam preparation. A suggested line that keeps the audience and refuses the claim: "Geen examencursus. Wel de woorden die je toch nodig hebt."

OPEN — owner to accept, adjust or overrule.

The promise that sells and can be checked

Proposed 16 November, in answer to "what do we promise that's sellable and measurable":

You will know more words every week than you did the week before — and you will be able to see it.

Two halves, both required:

  • Growth is the sellable half, and it must be a rate, not a total — see the trap below.
  • Proof is the measurable half. It is what lets us avoid a speed claim entirely and still say something strong.

The definition, to be wired into the product. A word counts when the learner answers it correctly in a review that comes at least three weeks after they last met it, having not seen it in between. That word is held. Everything the learner sees derives from that one rule:

Shown to the learner What it means
Words met seen at least once, in this theme or overall
Words held passed a review at a three-week gap
Words due held once, and the next review is now

Three rules that keep the number honest, because we are counting our own exercises:

  1. Never show the same sentence twice. Otherwise we measure memory for that sentence, not for the word.
  2. Randomise the distractors. A word is not held if it was the only plausible option.
  3. Count only at a real gap. Recognition in the same session is not memory.

The trap: a finite promise kills a subscription. "2,000 words and you're done" is a countdown to cancelling, and at €10/month the learner who cancels on completion is the worst case. So the promise has to be a rate, not a total — more words next week — which means the theme set stays effectively open-ended and later bands keep arriving. That is a standing commitment, not a slogan.

What we still may not promise: fluency, a CEFR level, an exam result, or a date.

Destination and promise are not the same sentence

  • The learner's destination is better fluency — holding a conversation without running out of words (§2). That is what we aim at, and it is honest to say we are working on the thing that stops them talking.
  • Our promise is the part a website can actually deliver: more words, held, every week.
  • Talking needs a person. The site does not supply one, which is why rung 3 exists (§5). So we may not say we will make someone fluent — only that we are fixing the words that fail them when they try.
  • The dashboard proves words, not conversation. Conversation comes from using the words on a human. Confusing the two is how competitors end up selling a guarantee they cannot keep.

4. Who it is for

An adult who has lived in the Netherlands a year or more, has done some Dutch already (a course, an intake, or Duolingo), and runs out of words in ordinary conversation. Practices on a phone, ten minutes at a time.

Confirmed by the owner, 16 November 2026.

What follows from it:

  • Adults who already live in the Netherlands, or are about to.
  • Already learning, not starting from zero. This is a supplement. Typically at or just past A2 — they finished a course and stopped there, which is the market in §2.
  • Most read English — but this must not be load-bearing (see §6).
  • Phone-first experience matters; assume small screen and short sessions.

The inburgering exam is out of scope — settled 16 November, reason given: it needs specialist knowledge (the exam's own formats, and what the municipality tests) that this product does not have and should not pretend to have.

The learners are still the audience; it is the exam that is out. So the site is what you use alongside an exam course, and the words are the words they need anyway. It is also a clean position to take in public, and one no exam course can copy: we are not competing with their results.

5. The ladder: passive → active → real world

This is the answer to "how does a word get from known to used", and it defines what the site does and where it stops.

Rung What happens What carries it State
1. Recognise The word is met in its theme, often enough to be known on sight and hearing. This is the target at launch. The games Built
2. Produce The learner has to use it — build a sentence, tell a story The Story Machine (Docs/story-machine.html) Exists; more producer games planned
3. Use it on a person We point them at real places to practise — a taalcafé in the library, a conversation group, a volunteer scheme A directory page Dropped 16 Nov — deferred, out of the current product

Notes:

  • The site's job ends at rung 2. Rung 3 is signposting, not teaching. We are not running a class and we are not stitching together strangers.
  • Rung 3 is dropped for now (owner, 16 November: out of scope). The consequence has to be said plainly: with no directory the product stops at rung 2, so copy may not promise conversation — only words. §3 already draws that line; this makes it mandatory rather than careful.
  • The directory is a product decision, not an appendix. It commits us to content that goes stale — places close, times change. OPEN: who owns it, how often it is checked, and whether it is one national page or per-city.
  • Passive first is deliberate. Active production needs a partner, a class or a teacher; the site can only simulate it. Rung 2 is the honest ceiling of what software alone can do, which is why rung 3 exists at all.

6. Language policy — a product rule, not a style choice

The site must work for someone who does not read English well.

  1. Dutch first, always. Every control, heading, instruction and error message is in Dutch, written in simple Dutch — short sentences, one idea each.
  2. English is a convenience, never a dependency. An English gloss sits beside the Dutch where it helps. If removing it breaks the task, the design is wrong.
  3. Meaning travels in picture, colour and shape. A learner should be able to do the exercise without parsing any sentence in any language.
  4. Exception: legal and account pages (privacy, voorwaarden, account mail) are in normal Dutch and normal English. They are contracts, not practice.
  5. No lookup detours. An unknown word is resolved in place — a tap, a picture, a gloss — and the exercise continues. Anything that sends the learner to a dictionary or a second tab has already lost them. This comes straight from the owner's own account: television plus a dictionary ends in zoning out.

Consequence to accept: "simple Dutch" is a second writing job on every string, not a translation of the English one.

OPEN — is there a floor below which we do not go, e.g. no Dutch word a complete beginner could not decode? Or do we accept A2 Dutch chrome?

7. Money and the gate

Settled:

  • Volume, not margin. 1,000 people at €10/month beats 10 at €50.
  • Cheap enough that price is not the reason someone says no. The learner who needs this most often has least.

The gate — settled 16 November

Who Gets Pays
Guest, no account Two themes (Boodschappen, Mensen), A1 and below, with every activity inside them. Nothing is remembered. —
Signed in, first 6 weeks Every theme, every band, the word log Free
After 6 weeks, and after any cancellation Back to the two free themes, with a log — frozen and read-only —
Paid Every theme, every band, the log at full scope, reviews everywhere €10/month or €100/year
  • One tier. No per-band pricing, no second subscription level, no add-ons. €100 for a year is the only discount — €8.33 a month against €10.
  • Six weeks, not four. §3 defines a word as held only when it comes back correctly after a three-week gap. A four-week trial expires at almost the first moment that evidence could exist, and only for someone who started on day one and never missed. Six weeks is the first length at which an ordinary starter can watch the product prove the one thing it promises.
  • The free tier keeps a log. After the trial a learner falls back to two themes with memory, not to a dead end. That is what makes €10 an upgrade — "more themes, more words" — rather than a wall. Nobody is asked to pay before they have watched the counter move.
  • The free slice is capped twice: two themes, and A1 and below (owner, 16 November). Both limits apply, so the taster is the first band of two themes.

The book is on hold

Owner, 16 November: the book is not part of the offer. It is sold separately, through Amazon.

  • €10/month and €100/year do not include it, and buying it grants nothing.
  • It was one acquisition channel, not the only one, and it is now unavailable. So it cannot be counted on: the site has to convert a cold visitor on its own. Working out where those visitors come from is an open question, not an emergency, and it is not part of this document yet.
  • Nothing in the product may depend on a book code or a purchase path that does not exist.

After a cancellation

Owner: the log stays, but "being careful not to make it easy for them to download them and use them for another purpose — we need them to come to the site if they want to learn them."

So: frozen, read-only and counted — words met, held and due, by theme. Not a word list with glosses, and no export. Honest caveat: anything on a screen can be copied by hand, so the goal is not to fight copying; it is to not hand over a tidy export that could stand in for the product.

What the gate costs to build

Access is one service today (GameAccessService), keyed by band and by position within a band. The gate above adds three dimensions: theme, account state (guest / trial / free / paid) and the log's scope. That is a change to the access rule itself, not a setting — and §12's theme navigation needs the theme axis anyway.

Also note §8: a guest has no log, so ten minutes of guest play accumulates nothing. That is a deliberate trade — it makes an account worth having on day one — but it means the taster has to stand up on its own, with no memory behind it.

What the price forces

Self-serve signup and cancellation with no human in the loop; no per-user support beyond a FAQ; no sales team; no per-learner content authoring. Anything added per learner has to cost near zero at 1,000 users and still cost near zero at 10,000.

8. The memory model: spaced repetition

Owner's answer, 16 November:

Review sessions at key points, and reviews at random times.

What that means concretely:

  • Sessions, not just streaks. A review session appears after a set amount of new material, and old words are interleaved into new sessions at intervals.
  • It needs per-learner state on the server. GET /api/games/{game}/progress exists; there is no scheduling behind it yet. This is the largest piece of work the product decision creates.
  • Session-level scheduling is enough to start. Per-word scheduling (each word with its own next-due date) is better and is what "spaced repetition" usually means, but a fixed interleave is a legitimate first version and can be replaced without changing the learner's experience.

The state is per word. Owner, 16 November. Not per chunk, not per theme — that is what the §3 definition of held requires, and it is the most work.

What the backend must hold

Owner, 16 November:

Keep a log of the words met, held, due, etc, and a log of what needs to be reviewed. We need that as a feature somewhere in the back end for those signed in.

Per signed-in learner, the server owns:

The log Contents
Word log one row per word: first met, times met, last met, whether held, when next due
Review queue derived from the word log — what is due now, oldest first
Session history enough to recompute the above, and to answer §9's questions

This is additive to the existing account system, not a new one: Identity, SQLite and sign-in already exist under server/, so this is new tables plus an endpoint. The harder part is not storage but the review scheduling rule.

The log keeps the picture. Owner, 16 November: "pictures are important to aid memory — our brains remember images better than words." So a review shows the image, not the English gloss. If the review shows the gloss, the gloss is what was learned and the picture was decoration. This also means the word row must carry a reference to its art, not only its text.

Two consequences:

  1. The log only exists for signed-in learners. A guest plays and learns nothing persists. That makes the account genuinely useful rather than a gate, and it is the honest basis for the free/paid line in §7.
  2. The log is also the proof in §3. Words met, held and due are not a marketing statistic kept internally — they are the learner's own screen.

9. How we know it works

OPEN — one or two numbers, checked at a fixed date. The candidates now follow from the §3 definition of held, and there should be one of each kind:

The product's own number — does the mechanism work:

  • Share of words met that are later held (passed at a three-week gap). If this is low, the review model in §8 is wrong.
  • Words held per active learner per month. The headline.

The business's number — do people stay and pay:

  • Retained learners at 30 days.
  • Paying learners who renew at month 2.

10. Non-goals

Draft, to confirm:

  • Not a course. No lessons, no curriculum, no certificate, no teacher.
  • Not a speed promise. No "fluent by Christmas" (§3).
  • Not exam prep. No exam formats, no practice tests, no KNM. Settled 16 November (§4): it needs specialist knowledge.
  • Not a dictionary or translator.
  • Not a booking service. The rung-3 directory lists places; it does not take bookings or run the group (§5).
  • Not a social network. OPEN — no profiles, comments or leaderboards?

11. Engagement is a constraint, not a flavour

Owner, 16 November:

People advise me to watch TV but I get too frustrated opening the dictionary for words I don't know and I zone out, so I want something more engaging. I'm actually the very first test subject to see if the system works, because I also have ADHD — so if this system can teach me and keep me engaged, that's the test.

What follows:

  • The named user has ADHD. Short sessions, an obvious next action, immediate feedback, and never leaving the exercise (§6, rule 5). These are ADHD-friendly and they are also simply good design — they do not turn the product into a niche product.
  • The acceptance test is the owner using it for weeks without stopping. Not a survey, not a mockup review. If he stops, that is the finding.
  • He is a fair test, not a flattering one. His A2 came after a year of courses to near B1 — that is precisely the learner in §4, not an expert early-adopter.
  • Engagement is not learning. Keeping him playing is this section; whether the words are held is §9. Both must be true and neither implies the other. A product that is fun and teaches nothing fails the same test as a boring one that works.
  • Streaks and reminders are not automatically dark patterns here. The usual objection to gamification is that it manufactures a need. When the user's real problem is starting, external structure helps. The line: structure that helps you begin is fine; guilt, loss-framing and nagging are not.
  • The tension with §2 stands. The mechanism is repetition, so the pleasure has to come from pace, feel and getting faster — not from novelty. A game that is different every time is a game the learner is not drilling.

What the mechanism is — owner, 16 November:

A reminder will be needed, and a sense of excitement, a visible and obvious progress — maybe a tally of how many words I've been exposed to and how many are learned.

Piece Status
A tally: words met vs words held Decided. It is the same counter as §3, so the promise and the progress display are one thing, not two.
A reminder Decided in principle; the channel is open.
"Excitement" A design goal, not a mechanism. It comes from pace, from the tally moving and from getting faster — not from confetti, and not from novelty (§2).

The reminder's honest costs:

  • Email is the only channel with infrastructure today. server/ already sends mail, and without an SMTP host it writes to email-sink/. Web push is unreliable on iOS, which is most of the audience.
  • A reminder is personal data processing. It needs explicit consent, and privacy.html currently states there are no accounts and no personal data. That page has to change before this ships.

The home page follows from this. If the first screen is a tally of words met and held, then the home is a counter plus an obvious next action — not six equal painted tiles. This is the structural answer to "the site doesn't match the product"; it is derived from the product, not from taste.

OPEN — which visible mechanism: a streak, a daily target, a visible next step, several of them, or none.

12. Content: words by theme

Owner's answer, 16 November:

We're building words in themes and I'm sure we can come up with the 2000 most commonly needed words, and more. There are no official word lists but we will build upon themes.

Consequences:

  • Themes are the navigation unit. Not a frequency list, not CEFR bands as the primary axis. A learner chooses a theme; the words come with it.
  • Completeness is measurable per theme, which is the answer to "are we done": a theme is done when its words are covered and have enough example contexts.
  • We choose themes; no authority chooses them for us. So the theme set is an editorial product decision and needs to be written down and defended once, not invented per batch.
  • Frequency lists will disagree with us. Where they do, we follow themes and say why — a word that is frequent but never needed in the learner's life is not our word.

Themes are the navigation, not just the content

Owner, 16 November:

We can organise the games also by theme. It will be a bit more complex but we can offer themes rather than just straight into games, and then guide them through a path of different games to practice that theme, so a theme will have to crosscut into different games.

So the learner chooses a theme, and the site walks them through a sequence of different games that all practise it. The games become tools inside a theme, not six doors on the home page.

Consequences:

  1. Two axes must be tagged independently. Every word has a theme; every exercise belongs to a game. A theme path is a sequence of (theme, game) combinations, and content has to carry both tags.
  2. The game's own internal progression becomes secondary. Entering by theme makes the band ladder inside a game a side door for people who want "more prepositions", not the main road.
  3. This answers "several games, different learning goals". The goals are the themes; the games are the tools. A separate skill taxonomy (Woorden/Spelling/Betekenis/Zinnen/Spreken) is no longer needed as the top axis.

Blocker to resolve: Voorzetselpaden is organised by chunk and CEFR band and its 713 chunks carry no theme tag. Either it gains theme tags — real content work — or it stays the one game reachable only by its own path. OPEN.

The theme set

Proposed 16 November; the owner called it "a good start" and added three needs.

Theme Covers Picture Free?
Boodschappen supermarket, prices, quantities, paying, waar staat de …? basket yes
Mensen small talk, invitations, feelings, birthdays two speech bubbles yes
Wonen house, neighbours, rent, repairs key no
Werk shift, tools, colleagues, safety toolbox no
Gezondheid doctor, pharmacy, body, appointment plaster no
Het lichaam body parts: head, arm, leg, back, knee, stomach, tooth a body outline no
Kinderen en school crèche, teacher, playground, notes school bag no
Gemeente en post letters, forms, DigiD, the system envelope no
Reizen bus, train, tickets, the city bus stop no
De weg asking, giving and following directions turning arrow no
Afspraken booking, moving, cancelling, arriving late calendar no

Two different kinds of unit were hiding in the answer. Themes are worlds — where the learner is. Directions, booking an appointment and asking where a product is are actions — what the learner does, and they crosscut worlds: an appointment happens at the doctor, at the gemeente and at the garage alike.

Resolution taken here, to be confirmed:

  • Asking where a product is lives inside Boodschappen — it is one chunk set, not a theme.
  • De weg and Afspraken are promoted to themes, because both are drawable, both are exactly the felt need, and both have enough vocabulary to carry a theme.
  • If more actions accumulate, a second axis gets made explicitly — later, and only when the list demands it.

Two themes are still the weakest as pictures even with their word beside them: Gemeente en post (an envelope says "post", not "the system") and Mensen (two speech bubbles say "talking", not "chatting with the neighbour"). Weak is not the same as unusable — the word carries the rest.

Size check: 2,000 words over 10 themes is ~200 each, still a month of practice per theme and a finishable unit.

Body parts are their own theme (owner, 16 November) rather than only a corner of Gezondheid: the vocabulary is large, it is in daily use well beyond the doctor, and it is the easiest theme in the set to draw — a body outline carries most of it. Worth keeping in mind if the free pair is ever revisited.

More themes — the owner wants them

Owner, 16 November: "We need more themes." Agreed, and it solves a problem: ten themes makes the free slice 20% of the product and "2,000 words" a total someone can finish — which fights the rate promise in §3.

Worlds — where the learner is

Theme Covers Picture

Actions — what the learner does

Theme Covers Picture

Cost, so the number is chosen rather than wished. A theme is roughly 200 words, one 512px pictogram, and a path through the games. 10 themes ≈ 2,000 words, 20 ≈ 4,000, 25 ≈ 5,000. Each theme added also shrinks the two free themes' share of the product — a gate decision, not only a content one.

Pictures all round — the owner is right. Every theme can be drawn. The picture does not have to carry the meaning alone: the Dutch word sits with it, so the drawing only has to get the learner close. Emergency vehicles for Nood en politie; a traditional handset for Bellen against a smartphone for Telefoon en internet; a form and a pen for Formulieren against an envelope for Gemeente en post.

The one thing no picture plus word can carry is grammar — ik werk versus ik heb gewerkt, word order, op versus aan. Those are relations, not things, which is why grammar lives inside exercises (and why Voorzetselpaden needed scenes rather than icons).

OPEN — how many themes, and which of the above.

The count

10 base + 13 candidates + Het lichaam = 24 themes. That is:

  • 24 drawable themes — the picture plus the Dutch word together identify each one; no theme is left without a picture.
  • roughly 4,800 words at ~200 per theme — past A2 and into B1 territory, which is consistent with §2's "2,000 and more" but is a large authoring job.
  • a free slice of 2 of 24, about 8% of the product.

The set can be defined now and shipped over time — but a theme shown as a door must have content behind it, so the visible set at launch is the content budget, not the target.

Words are global; paths are local. With 24 overlapping themes the same word arrives in several places — de rekening in Boodschappen and Eten en drinken, de knie in Het lichaam and Gezondheid. That is good for practice and a trap for counting: the word log in §8 must be keyed by word, not by (theme, word), or a word met in two themes is counted twice and the counter in §3 lies.

Every theme hosts every activity that fits it

Goal set 16 November: a theme is not a word list, it is a path of different games. So each theme should carry every activity that makes sense for its words.

Where a game genuinely does not fit a theme — a preposition game may have nothing to say about Klachten — that is acceptable, but it must be a recorded decision, not an accident, and the theme's path still needs enough variety to feel like a path rather than the same drill relabelled.
