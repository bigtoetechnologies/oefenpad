raak · mis

hit or miss · 2 cards

Zijn eerste worp is raak, maar de tweede is mis.His first throw is on target, but the second one is off target.

Fixed frame. One plain round target board of five rings, no numbers, hangs on a plain wall at head height, seen from the front, with one plain dart in flight or landed. Only where the dart ends changes: in the centre ring, or in the wall beside the board.

#30
geen beeld

raak

on target

hit or missA2

Scene
The plain round target board with five rings hangs on the plain wall, and one plain dart is stuck with its tip in the centre ring, the shaft sticking out straight toward the viewer. The rest of the board and the wall around it are bare.
Must read
the dart's tip in the centre of the rings.
Avoid
a dart in the wall beside the board (mis); a dart in the outer ring (bijna); a dart in the air with no target (not the card); a person throwing a dart (not the frame).
Contrast
Pair with mis. The only difference is where the dart ends: in the centre ring here, in the bare wall beside the board there.
Example
Het schot is raak!
The shot is on target!
Alt text
Een pijl steekt in het midden van een ronde schijf met cirkels.

omhoog · omlaag

De ballon gaat omhoog, maar de bal gaat omlaag.The balloon goes up, but the ball goes down.

Fixed frame. One child stands whole-figure in side view on a flat ground line, head tilted back, with one plain round ball and a plain wall behind, same figure size on the page. Only the ball changes: rising away from the child's hands, or dropping toward them.

#32
geen beeld

omhoog

up

direction of movementA2

Scene
One child stands side-on on the ground line with arms stretched straight up and head tilted back, hands just opened after a throw. A plain round ball hangs high above the hands, three short strokes below it marking its upward path, and the plain wall behind is bare.
Must read
the ball high above the hands with strokes beneath it and the face tilted up.
Avoid
a ball dropping toward the hands with strokes above it (omlaag); a tall tower (hoog); a ball resting on a shelf (boven); a child jumping with the ball in hand (not the card).
Contrast
Pair with omlaag. The only difference is the ball's path: strokes below it as it rises away from the hands here, strokes above it as it falls toward the hands there.
Example
Kijk omhoog, daar vliegt een vogel.
Look up, there is a bird flying.
Alt text
Een kind met de armen gestrekt kijkt naar een bal ver boven zijn handen met drie streepjes eronder.

Fixed frame. Scene
The plain three-way junction in a high three-quarter view from behind the adult, who stands at its foot; a small house stands beside the left branch. A dotted line runs from the adult's feet straight up the middle path to the top edge, while the left and right branches carry no line.

a dotted line running straight up the middle branch.

a dotted line turning into the left branch (linksaf); a dotted line turning into the right branch (rechtsaf); a dotted line that loops back (not the card); a long straight road with no junction (lang).

Fixed frame. Six identical plain wooden blocks lie on a plain table seen from the front, a plain wall behind, same block size on the page. Only the arrangement of the blocks changes: all gathered into one spot, or spread out one by one.

#39
geen beeld

arrangement of blocksA2

Scene
The six plain wooden blocks are stacked and pushed together into one tight cluster in the middle of the table, every block touching at least one other, the outline of the group compact. The rest of the table and the wall behind are bare.

Fixed frame. A straight forest path runs up the page, seen from high above, with a row of tree trunks along each edge. One fallen trunk, longer than the path is wide, lies on the path with its ends between the verge trees. Only the angle of the fallen trunk changes.

#43
geen beeld

dwars

across (at a right angle)

angle of a trunk over a pathA2

Scene
The straight forest path is seen from a high viewpoint. The fallen tree trunk lies at a right angle to the path, from one edge straight to the other, closing the whole width like a gate. The rows of tree trunks along both edges are unchanged and nothing else is on the path.
Must read
a trunk lying at a right angle across the path.
Avoid
a trunk lying at a slant across the path (schuin); a trunk lying along the path (not the card); a tree still standing at the edge (rechtop); a pile of cut logs (not the card).
Contrast
Pair with schuin. The only difference is the angle: the trunk lies at a right angle to the path here, at a slant there. Cross-reference: Voorzetselpaden B1+ draws a tree dwars over de weg and one car dwars in a row of schuin parked cars; the taught items differ.

Alt text
